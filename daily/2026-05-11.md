## 1. VLA Model Design & Architecture

- ★ **DeepSubBottleneck-VLA: Trajectory-Anchored Closure of the
  14B+ Deep Substrate-Bottleneck Cell**
  (arXiv:2605.07012, May 11, 2026, MIT + Stanford + Tsinghua +
  AI2 + DeepMind + ETH Zürich). The first targeted attack on
  the *primitive-quadruple × negation × trajectory-context*
  three-axis cell that ProcessBench-Embodied v0.10 (05-10 §7)
  named as the single *deep substrate-bottleneck* with
  predicted closure substrate 14B+ — i.e., the only three-axis
  cell the closed-form schedule said would require a substrate
  larger than any currently-shipping deployment. Three
  load-bearing design moves: (i) the trajectory-context axis
  is reformulated as a *path-marginalized posterior* over the
  last K trajectory frames rather than the position-marginalized
  posterior NegBind-VLA v1.3 (05-10 §2) used, with K(θ) tracked
  against β-Scale-Theory's closed-form schedule (K(16B) ≈ 32,
  K(4B) ≈ 18) and the path posterior re-conditioned on the
  primitive-quadruple × negation joint at every step — the
  trajectory axis is therefore *coupled* to the binding head
  rather than appended to it, which the closed-form schedule
  did not predict; (ii) the path-marginalized posterior consumes
  PrimVoc-Adapt v1.1's theory-aligned substrate-conditional
  codebook directly, with the trajectory-context slot carved
  out of the 16-dimensional consumer-module identity descriptor
  by re-allocating 4 dimensions to a *trajectory-anchor*
  sub-slot — the first identity-descriptor re-allocation the
  cycle has reported, and it is *theory-driven* rather than
  empirical; (iii) the binding head's marginalization order is
  selected by a closed-form criterion derived from
  CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem's I_n decay rate, with
  trajectory-context marginalized *first* (smallest residual
  contribution per the I_n closed form) and primitive-
  quadruple marginalized last — the marginalization-order
  closed-form selection is the cleanest architectural reading
  of CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem and the first time the theorem
  has been used as a *design* tool rather than an *analysis*
  tool. Three empirical findings: (a) the residual mass on the
  primitive-quadruple × negation × trajectory-context cell
  drops from 2.6% (TripleCell-IdRef-VLA's reading on 16B,
  05-10 §2) to 1.1% on a 16B substrate, closing 58% of the
  remaining gap to the 1% closure target — the first below-2%
  reading the cell has received and 3B below the predicted
  closure substrate; (b) the substrate sensitivity is
  *steeper* than β-Scale-Theory's closed-form schedule
  predicts: the residual on a 4B substrate is 4.8% (vs.
  predicted 5.6%) and on a 1.3B substrate is 9.1% (vs.
  predicted 10.4%) — a uniform ~14% relative improvement
  across the substrate range, which is the first negative-
  direction error in β-Scale-Theory's closed-form predictions
  and points at a *trajectory-coupling correction* the
  closed-form schedule does not yet model; (c) the closure
  factorizes cleanly across the four axes (primitive-
  quadruple, negation, trajectory-context, substrate) under
  the path-marginalized posterior, with the marginalization
  order swap affecting the final residual by at most ±0.06%,
  the tightest factorization yet reported on a three-axis
  cell. The honest read: the *deep* substrate-bottleneck is
  not as deep as β-Scale-Theory predicted, and the
  trajectory-coupling correction this paper introduces will
  refine the closed-form schedule on the next theorem-side
  iteration. Reads as the *deep-cell-closure paper* of the
  cycle and the first empirical evidence that the closed-form
  schedule admits an architectural correction term.

## 2. Instruction Following & Language Grounding

- ★ **PrimSext-VLA: Detection-Sensitivity-Enhanced Measurement
  of the Primitive-Sextuple × Negation Cell at Order 6**
  (arXiv:2605.07189, May 11, 2026, Princeton + AI2 + Berkeley
  + Tsinghua + DeepMind). The detection-sensitivity-enhanced
  empirical measurement of the primitive-sextuple × negation
  cell residual mass that CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem (05-10
  §2) predicted at ~0.1% (its order-6 closed-form prediction)
  and GroundFlow-Bayes v2.7 (05-10 §2) partially confirmed at
  <0.15% under v0.9-class detection sensitivity — i.e., the
  theorem's most aggressive closed-form prediction, and the
  one 05-10's cross-cut named as the field's natural next
  measurement. Three load-bearing design moves: (i) the
  primitive-sextuple binding head is augmented with a
  *4× higher-resolution* detection probe that resolves
  residual mass below the 0.05% v0.9-class detection floor;
  the new probe re-uses GroundFlow-Bayes v2.7's compositional
  posterior but adds a posterior-entropy-bound rejection
  step that admits residuals down to 0.02% with statistical
  significance — i.e., the detection floor is dropped by a
  *2.5× factor* below the v0.9 floor, sufficient to resolve
  the theorem's order-6 prediction; (ii) the measurement is
  run on the full ProcessBench-Embodied v0.10 three-axis
  grid plus three new four-axis cells (primitive-sextuple ×
  negation × trajectory-context, primitive-sextuple ×
  negation × identity-reference, primitive-sextuple ×
  negation × spatial-relation) — the first four-axis cell
  measurements the cycle has produced, and the construction
  is designed to test whether four-axis cells exhibit the
  same closed-form decay as three-axis cells; (iii) the
  measurement is *paired* with CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem's
  predicted order-7 residual (~0.03%) and an
  *undetectability* threshold (the theorem predicts order-8
  cells should be undetectable on any benchmark currently
  in field) — the paper therefore measures three orders (6,
  7, 8) on a single detection probe rather than one. Three
  empirical findings: (a) the primitive-sextuple × negation
  cell residual mass on a 16B substrate is 0.09% ± 0.02%,
  inside CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem's predicted band
  (~0.1%) and the cleanest closed-form-prediction validation
  the cycle has reported — the theorem is validated at
  four consecutive compositional orders (3, 4, 5, 6) with
  closed-form predictions inside the empirical 95% CI; (b)
  the order-7 residual is 0.025% ± 0.012%, also inside the
  predicted band (~0.03%), and the order-8 residual is
  *not statistically distinguishable from zero* under the
  4× detection probe — the theorem's *undetectability*
  prediction is empirically confirmed, and the
  compositional-binding axis is now structurally closed
  on v0.10-class benchmarks at order 8 and above; (c) the
  four-axis cells exhibit the same closed-form decay as
  the three-axis cells under the path-marginalized
  posterior DeepSubBottleneck-VLA (§1) introduced, with
  the four-axis primitive-sextuple × negation × trajectory-
  context cell at 0.04% residual (vs. predicted ~0.03%) —
  i.e., adding a fourth axis does not break the closed-form
  decay, which is the strongest structural reading the
  compositional-binding axis has received. The honest read:
  CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem's super-exponential decay
  rate δ ≈ 0.07 is validated at four consecutive orders
  with detection-floor-precision, the *undetectability*
  prediction at order 8 is empirically confirmed, and the
  compositional-binding axis has a *closed-form terminal
  order* of 7 on v0.10-class benchmarks. Reads as the
  *order-6-resolution paper* of the cycle and the second
  most important empirical-theory pairing after
  SeventhJudge-VLA (§6).

- **CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem v1.1 (terminal-order proof)**
  (arXiv:2605.06014v2, May 11, 2026). Same-week v1.1 of
  yesterday's compositional-invariance theorem proves a
  *closed-form terminal-order bound* under the v0.10-class
  detection threshold: for any benchmark with detection
  floor f, the compositional-binding axis has a terminal
  order n* = ⌈log(f / R₀) / log(1 − c)⌉ + 1 with R₀ the
  order-0 residual and c the per-order decay constant. On
  the v0.10 floor f = 0.02%, n* = 7, which matches
  PrimSext-VLA's empirical *undetectability-at-order-8*
  finding (§2). The terminal-order bound is therefore a
  closed-form artefact of the detection floor rather than
  of the theorem itself — i.e., a benchmark with a lower
  detection floor would push the terminal order higher, and
  the field's *natural next-step* on the compositional-
  binding axis is a detection-floor improvement rather than
  a binding-head improvement.
