## 1. VLA Model Design & Architecture

- ★ **β-Scale-Theory: An Information-Theoretic Derivation of the
  Cross-Axis Substrate Exponent**
  (arXiv:2605.06127, May 10, 2026, Stanford + DeepMind +
  EPFL + Tsinghua + Toyota Research Institute + Westlake). The
  formalization paper for the cross-axis β ≈ 0.4 substrate
  exponent that 05-09's cross-cut named as the cycle's most
  important open theoretical question. Five-axis empirical
  evidence had accumulated by 05-09 (codebook scaling, cross-cut
  coupling, memory retrieval, world-model calibration,
  open-cell residual mass), but no derivation tied the exponent
  to a substrate primitive. β-Scale-Theory closes that loop.
  Three load-bearing design moves: (i) the substrate's
  effective head dimension θ is decomposed as θ = d · h with d
  the per-head dimension and h the head count, and the
  primitive-pair mutual-information rate I(P; θ) is shown to
  satisfy I(P; θ) = I₀ · (θ / θ₀)^(1/2) − ε(θ) with ε(θ) a
  bounded approximation residual — i.e., the mutual-information
  rate scales at √θ, not θ, which is the cleanest single
  observation in the paper; (ii) the channel-capacity-limited
  capability gain on a primitive-pair-keyed channel is then
  shown to scale as (θ / θ₀)^β with β = (1/2) − γ where γ is
  the *codebook-entropy-loss correction* that PrimVoc-Adapt's
  α(θ) schedule introduces, with γ ≈ 0.08 ± 0.02 across the
  fitted substrate range — the prediction is therefore
  β = 0.42 ± 0.03, in agreement with PrimVoc-Adapt's empirical
  β ≈ 0.42 (05-09 §1) to within the theoretical bound; (iii)
  the derivation is *axis-agnostic*: the same β applies to any
  substrate-resolution-limited channel (cross-cut coupling,
  memory retrieval, world-model calibration, open-cell residual
  mass) provided the channel's capacity is mutual-information-
  bounded, which is the structural reading 05-09's cross-cut
  conjectured but did not prove. Three empirical findings
  validate the theory: (a) the closed-form β = 0.42 lies inside
  the 95% confidence interval of the empirical β on every one
  of the five 05-09 axes (codebook 0.42 ± 0.03, cross-cut
  0.40 ± 0.04, memory 0.41 ± 0.05, world-model 0.39 ± 0.04,
  open-cell 0.43 ± 0.05); (b) the predicted *substrate-relative
  capability ratio* of 2.4×-per-decade (05-09 cross-cut)
  matches the empirical ratio 2.39 ± 0.05 across 31 LBM Eval
  v3.4 leaderboard entries; (c) a *predicted sixth axis* — the
  meta-judge stable-iteration ceiling — should also exhibit
  β ≈ 0.4 scaling under the theory, and the paper reports a
  retroactive fit on the BiTrajJudge-VLA (05-09 §6) per-substrate
  ceilings that returns β = 0.40 ± 0.06, which is the first
  evidence that the meta-judge ceiling axis joins the
  substrate-resolution-limited family. The honest read: the
  field's primary capability channels are *mutual-information-
  bounded at √θ* with a bounded codebook-entropy-loss
  correction, and the substrate-resolution limit is now a
  closed-form theoretical fact rather than an empirical
  curiosity. Reads as the *substrate-theory paper* of the cycle
  and the most important single-paper formalization the
  smaller-substrate community has received in 2026.

- ★ **CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem: A Closed-Form Compositional
  Invariance for Negation under Primitive Binding**
  (arXiv:2605.06014, May 10, 2026, AI2 + CMU + Princeton +
  Tsinghua + Berkeley + DeepMind). The theorem paper for the
  negation-vs-binding compositional invariance result
  NegBind-VLA (05-07 §2), v1.1 (05-08 §2), and PrimQuadNeg-VLA
  (05-09 §2) confirmed empirically at three compositional
  orders. 05-09 explicitly named the formalization as the
  field's natural next step; CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem
  delivers it. Three load-bearing design moves: (i) the
  marginal-vs-conditional asymmetry between negation N and
  primitive binding B is formalized as a *compositional
  invariance* I_n satisfying N ⊗ B^(⊗n) = N ⊗ B^(⊗(n−1)) ⊗ B
  with a residual term R_n that vanishes in the limit n → ∞;
  the paper proves R_n = O((1 − c)^(1+δ·n)) with δ ≈ 0.07,
  i.e., R_n decays *super-exponentially* in compositional order
  with a closed-form rate that is testable on the existing
  ProcessBench-Embodied open-cell data; (ii) the proof is
  *substrate-agnostic* and applies to any vocabulary in which
  the primitive-binding head admits a marginalized posterior,
  which subsumes the v1, v2, and Adapt vocabularies as special
  cases — the invariance is therefore a structural property of
  the primitive-vocabulary-binding pair rather than of any
  particular K or α schedule; (iii) the theorem yields a
  *predicted closure curve* for higher-order open cells: the
  primitive-quintuple × negation cell's residual mass should
  drop from PrimQuadNeg-VLA's 0.8% (at n = 4) to 0.3% (at
  n = 5) to 0.1% (at n = 6), with the field's empirical
  closure schedule now anchored to a closed-form prediction.
  Three empirical validations: (a) GroundFlow-Bayes v2.6's
  quintuple result (05-09 §2, curve constant 0.71) sits
  exactly on the I₅ invariance prediction (theoretical 0.713,
  empirical 0.71 ± 0.02), with the closed-form prediction
  inside the empirical 95% CI; (b) the (1 − c)^0.7 scaling
  law's curve constant 0.71 is shown to be *the same* constant
  as the theorem's δ ≈ 0.07 in a unit-corrected presentation —
  i.e., the data-side scaling law and the compositional
  invariance theorem are *the same closed-form law* observed
  along orthogonal axes, which is the strongest unification
  result the cycle has produced; (c) the predicted residual
  mass at order 6 (~0.1%) is below ProcessBench-Embodied's
  current detection threshold, so the theorem predicts the
  primitive-sextuple × negation cell will be *undetectable* on
  v0.9-class benchmarks — a falsifiable prediction that will
  drive the next benchmark-side adjustment. Reads as the
  *compositional-invariance paper* of the cycle and the first
  closed-form unification of the data-side scaling law and the
  compositional-binding axis.

   CompOrd-Invariance-Theorem (§2) names are *three
