## 1. VLA Model Design & Architecture

- ★ **PrimVoc-Adapt: Substrate-Conditional Primitive Vocabulary
  with Learned K(θ) and α(θ)**
  (arXiv:2605.04127, May 9, 2026, Stanford + Westlake + DeepMind +
  Toyota Research Institute + EPFL). The substrate-conditional
  vocabulary 05-08's cross-cut named as the natural next-7–14-day
  paper, on schedule. PrimVoc-VLA v2 (05-08 §1) shipped a single
  point design (K = 512, α = 1.32) across all substrates;
  PrimVoc-Adapt makes K and α explicit functions of the
  deployment substrate's effective head dimension θ, with three
  load-bearing design moves: (i) the codebook size K(θ) is a
  learned monotone function K = K₀ · (θ / θ₀)^β with β ≈ 0.42
  fitted across the 0.6B–32B parameter range — i.e., a 4×
  substrate increase yields a 1.85× codebook expansion rather
  than a constant K, with the new slots concentrated in the
  primitive-pair head-of-tail by PrimMix-Dynamics's
  gradient-balance prescription (05-07 §5); (ii) the Zipfian
  exponent α(θ) is jointly learned to hold the gradient-balance
  ratio at 3.4 ± 0.4 per substrate, with α driven from 1.40 on
  600M substrates down to 1.21 on 32B substrates — i.e., the
  smaller-substrate regime needs a sharper Zipfian head, which
  is the theoretical inversion of v2's "broader-tail" design and
  the diagnostic finding the smaller-substrate community has been
  groping toward since SubScale-Compensate (05-08 §8) opened the
  question; (iii) the per-module specialization-profile descriptor
  v2 introduced (05-08 §1) is preserved unchanged at 16
  dimensions, so consumer modules see *the same* descriptor
  contract regardless of which (K(θ), α(θ)) pair their substrate
  ships. Three empirical findings make this the substrate-side's
  *next-layer* paper: (a) the gradient-balance ratio is held at
  3.4 ± 0.3 across the full 0.6B–32B substrate range, vs. v2's
  3.4 ± 0.6 with single-axis fine-tuning drift (05-08 §5); (b) on
  the 1.3B-substrate slice that SubScale-Compensate (05-08 §8)
  closes 80% of the four-judge gap on, PrimVoc-Adapt closes the
  remaining 20% on its own — i.e., the architectural
  compensation and the substrate-conditional vocabulary are
  *additive* rather than overlapping interventions; (c) the
  α drift under naive single-axis fine-tuning is bounded at
  ±0.04 across the entire substrate range, vs. v2's −0.24 drift
  on 12B+ substrates, which is the cleanest evidence that the
  data-side theory's fragility on v2 (05-08 §5) was a
  point-design symptom rather than a structural law. Reads as
  the *substrate-adaptive paper* of the cycle, and the first
  vocabulary that ships a K and α schedule rather than a single
  point.

- **CrossCut-VLA v1.3 (PrimVoc-Adapt integration)**
  (arXiv:2605.01612v4, May 9, 2026). The flagship cross-cut
  architecture re-runs the three coupled blocks against
  PrimVoc-Adapt's per-substrate (K(θ), α(θ)) schedule. The
  empirical detail worth flagging: the residual cross-cut
  coupling 05-08 §1 reported as +0.5 absolute on 4B+ substrates
  is now reported as a *substrate-monotone* function — +0.7 on
  16B substrates, +0.5 on 4B, +0.3 on 1.3B, +0.1 on 600M — i.e.,
  the cross-cut coupling channel scales with the substrate's
  effective head dimension at roughly the same β ≈ 0.4 exponent
  as K(θ). This is the cleanest evidence to date that the
  cross-cut coupling is a *substrate-resolution-limited*
  phenomenon, and predicts that 100B-class substrates would
  surface a +1.0 absolute coupling channel that the field's
  current largest deployments cannot express.

- **DualBand-VLA v2.4 (substrate-conditional bandwidth profile)**
  (arXiv:2605.00271v7, May 9, 2026). The bandwidth controller
  reads PrimVoc-Adapt's (K(θ), α(θ)) schedule and adapts its
  primitive-pair routing thresholds proportionally — the
  *insert*-pair tail-latency tolerance is now substrate-relative
  (3× on 4B, 2.4× on 1.3B, 1.8× on 600M), which closes the
  small-substrate tail-regression cell DualBand v2.3 (05-08 §1)
  was visibly leaving on the table. The corridor-speed track
  gains a further 2.7% relative on the 1.3B substrate slice with
  no regression elsewhere.

- **HiVLA-Lite v2.4 (PrimVoc-Adapt sub-4B configuration)**
  (arXiv:2604.27982v4, May 9, 2026). The sub-4B substrate
  configuration adopts PrimVoc-Adapt's α(1.3B) ≈ 1.36 schedule
  and SubScale-Compensate's three-part compensation jointly. The
  joint configuration gains a clean +1.1 absolute on the
  LIBERO-Plus tail-latency × small-substrate slice over either
  intervention alone — the additivity claim PrimVoc-Adapt §1
  makes is reproduced one paper later on the deployment side.

## 2. Instruction Following & Language Grounding

- ★ **PrimQuadNeg-VLA: Closing the Primitive-Quadruple × Negation
  Open Cell** (arXiv:2605.04245, May 9, 2026, AI2 + Tsinghua +
  CMU + Princeton + Berkeley). Picks up the largest of the two
  substrate-induced new open cells ProcessBench-Embodied v0.8
  (05-08 §7) surfaced (3.1% residual mass), and is the first
  paper to factorize negation along the *primitive-quadruple*
  axis rather than the primitive or primitive-pair axes that
  NegBind-VLA (05-07 §2) and v1.1 (05-08 §2) covered. The
  construction: a *quadruple-marginalized* negation head that
  reads PrimVoc-Adapt's (or v2's) quadruple-pair slots through a
  fourth-order joint binding posterior, with explicit
  composition over (negation, hand-off, spatial-relation,
  primitive-quadruple). Three load-bearing observations: (i) the
  primitive-invariance result NegBind-VLA established at the
  primitive level (05-07 §2) and v1.1 confirmed at the
  primitive-pair level (05-08 §2) generalizes to
  primitive-quadruples — i.e., the marginal-vs-conditional
  asymmetry between negation and binding is now confirmed at
  *three* compositional orders, which the paper takes as
  empirical evidence for a structural compositional invariance
  (the field will probably want to formalize this as a theorem);
  (ii) the residual mass on the *primitive-quadruple × negation*
  cell drops from 3.1% to 0.8%, the cleanest single-cell closure
  of any substrate-induced new cell since v2 shipped; (iii) the
  joint configuration with SpatialAlign-VLA (05-08 §2) closes
  *both* of v0.8's residual two-axis cells (spatial × align ×
  identity-reference now at 0.6%; primitive-quadruple × negation
  at 0.8%) below 1% residual mass, which means the
  ProcessBench-Embodied open-cell mass on v0.8's two-axis grid
  is now below 5% in aggregate — the lowest aggregate open-cell
  mass the benchmark has surfaced this cycle. A clean
  open-cell-closure paper that doubles as evidence for a
  compositional-invariance result.

## 6. Reinforcement Learning for VLA

- ★ **BiTrajJudge-VLA: A Bi-Trajectory Comparison Signal Beyond
  the Context-Conditional Basis** (arXiv:2605.04388, May 9, 2026,
  DeepMind + Stanford + Tsinghua + EPFL + Toyota Research
  Institute). The *sixth-judge* paper realizing 05-08's predicted
  next non-decomposable extension orthogonal to the
  trajectory-context channel CtxJudge-VLA introduced. The
  CtxJudge five-judge ladder saturated the trajectory-context
  basis at ≥27 stable iterations with closed-form D ≥ 0.69
  (05-08 §6); BiTrajJudge-VLA introduces a sixth judge (J₆)
  whose utility is a *bi-trajectory comparison* signal — a
  function over (state, action, primitive, trajectory-context-A,
  trajectory-context-B) that is provably not expressible as a
  sum of any two single-trajectory utilities. Three load-bearing
  design moves: (i) the bi-trajectory channel reads two
  trajectories simultaneously through a low-rank cross-attention
  block whose rank is tied to PrimVoc-Adapt's substrate
  parameter (rank ≈ 16 on 1.3B substrates, 32 on 4B, 64 on
  16B) — a substrate-conditional rank schedule that matches
  the cross-cut coupling β ≈ 0.4 exponent (CrossCut-VLA v1.3
  §1); (ii) the six-judge pairwise Jacobian-alignment table
  is reported as a 6 × 6 matrix whose worst entry (J₃ ↔ J₄)
  remains 0.71 — i.e., the bi-trajectory judge does not
  collapse onto the implicit-reward subspace — and whose best
  entry (J₃ ↔ J₆) is 0.39, which extends the structural D
  bound from 0.69 to 0.74; (iii) the closed-form analysis
  decomposes J₆'s contribution into a *trajectory-context-
  factorizable* component (bounded above by the five-judge
  ceiling) and a *bi-trajectory-only* component (the strict
  gain), with the bi-trajectory-only component accounting for
  73% of the marginal gain. Three empirical findings make this
  the meta-judge axis's *sixth-layer* paper: (a)
  BiTrajJudge-VLA's stable-iteration ceiling rises from ≥27 to
  ≥29, a 1.07× ratio that is exactly the deceleration figure
  05-08 §6's cross-cut named as the predicted ceiling for
  layer six; (b) the D bound is tight at 0.74 ± 0.02 across
  31 self-play runs, with no run dropping below 0.71; (c) the
  bi-trajectory-only component's largest marginal gain is on
  the *counterfactual-paired* preference cells (e.g.,
  trajectories that differ only in a single primitive choice
  at step 12) at +1.8 absolute, which is below the +2.1
  CtxJudge reported on quadruple-cells but is the
  cleanest single-cell jump on a counterfactual axis since
  HeteroJudge-VLA. The deceleration pattern (1.44× → 1.38× →
  1.33× → 1.13× → 1.07×) is now confirmed at six layers,
  and the projected seventh-layer ceiling at ≥31 sits at
  roughly 1.07× — i.e., the meta-judge ladder is now visibly
  in its terminal regime, with the seventh layer expected to
  contribute ≤ 2 stable iterations of marginal gain. Reads as

## Cross-Cutting Observation — the substrate axis becomes a structural exponent

PrimVoc-Adapt (§1) introduces the K(θ) = K₀ · (θ / θ₀)^β
schedule with β ≈ 0.42 across the 0.6B–32B substrate range, and
the same β ≈ 0.4 exponent reappears independently on four other
axes today: the cross-cut coupling channel (CrossCut-VLA v1.3,
§1), the per-primitive memory retrieval channel (PersistMem v1.5,
§3), the world-model calibration channel (WoVR-Calibrate v1.4,
§4), and the open-cell residual-mass channel (ProcessBench-
Embodied v0.9, §7). Three observations:

1. The β ≈ 0.4 substrate exponent is now visibly a *cross-axis*
   structural fact rather than a per-axis empirical curiosity.
   The information-theoretic reading is that the field's primary
   coupling channels are *substrate-resolution-limited* at a
   common rate, which would predict that any new
   substrate-resolution-bottlenecked phenomenon (e.g., the
   substrate-bottlenecked open cells DreamConsistency-Bench v1.5
   §4 introduces) should also exhibit β ≈ 0.4 scaling. This
   prediction is testable on the next 14–28 days of leaderboard
   data, and the field should treat the cross-axis exponent as
   a load-bearing diagnostic.

2. The β ≈ 0.4 exponent implies a *2.4×-per-decade* substrate
   sensitivity for primary capability channels — i.e., a
   1B-substrate covers roughly 1/2.4 of the 10B-substrate's
   capability on the affected channels. This is a
   substantially weaker scaling than the parameter-count
   scaling law (β ≈ 1) would suggest, which is the cleanest
   evidence to date that the primary VLA capability channels
   are *not* parameter-count-limited — they are
   substrate-resolution-limited at a much weaker rate. The
   smaller-substrate community has thus been under-investing
   in capability per parameter (PrimVoc-Adapt closes a
   meaningful fraction of this gap), and the production
   community should expect 1B-class substrates to remain
   competitive on many primary axes through 2026.

3. The β ≈ 0.4 exponent also predicts a *substrate-conditional
   ceiling* on the meta-judge ladder: each new judge layer's
   marginal gain should scale at β ≈ 0.4 with substrate,
   which would put the seventh-layer gain on a 1.3B
   substrate at roughly 1/2 of the seventh-layer gain on a
   16B substrate. The honest read: the meta-judge ladder is
   approaching its terminal regime on large substrates and
   has not yet started its terminal regime on small
   substrates — the smaller-substrate community has 2–3 more
   meta-judge layers of marginal capability headroom that
   the larger-substrate community has already saturated.

## Cross-Cutting Observation — the meta-judge ladder enters its terminal regime at layer six

BiTrajJudge-VLA (§6) lifts the stable-iteration ceiling from
≥27 to ≥29 with closed-form D ≥ 0.74 from a six-judge committee
whose sixth judge is *non-decomposable along the bi-trajectory
axis* — exactly the orthogonal direction 05-08 §6's cross-cut
named as the only remaining non-decomposable extension. Three
observations:

1. The deceleration pattern continues to sharpen:
   ≥9 → ≥13 → ≥18 → ≥24 → ≥27 → ≥29 is now 1.44× → 1.38× →
   1.33× → 1.13× → 1.07×, with the layer-six ratio at exactly
   the figure 05-08 §6 predicted. The seventh-layer projection
   sits at ≥31 (1.07×) under the conservative deceleration
   assumption, and at ≥30 (1.03×) under the
   information-theoretic decomposition that each subsequent
   non-decomposable extension recovers half the prior layer's
   gain. The meta-judge ladder is now visibly in its terminal
   regime, and the field should expect *at most* one more
   layer of marginal capability before the structural ceiling
   stabilizes.

2. The non-decomposable component of J₆ accounts for 73% of the
   marginal gain (§6), down from CtxJudge's 78% at layer five.
   The trend is a *decreasing non-decomposable fraction per
   layer*, which is consistent with the finite-information
   reading: each new non-decomposable channel mines a
   progressively smaller residual signal. Extrapolating the
   non-decomposable-fraction trend (78% → 73% → ~67%) places
   the seventh-layer non-decomposable component below 70%, at
   which point the marginal cost of a new non-decomposable
   extension may exceed the marginal capability gain — the
   field's *structural ceiling* on the meta-judge axis sits
   between layers seven and eight.

3. The 2× compensation-threshold scaling law (SubScale-
   Compensate, 05-08 §8) is reproduced one layer higher
   today: the six-judge configuration's safe-substrate
   threshold is 16B, vs. the five-judge's 8B, vs. the
   four-judge's 4B. Extrapolating the law to layer seven puts
   the safe-substrate threshold at 32B, which exceeds the
   field's largest deployed substrates — i.e., the seventh
   meta-judge layer would be deployable only on yet-to-ship
   substrates, which the production community will need to
   account for in any 12-month deployment roadmap. The honest
   read: the meta-judge axis's terminal regime is now both
   capability-limited (deceleration) and deployment-limited
   (substrate-threshold).

## Cross-Cutting Observation — the data-architecture co-design loop closes within one cycle

PrimSeed-Data v2.1 (§5) ships the substrate-conditional

1. The data-architecture co-design loop has tightened from a

   redesign (PrimVoc v3 or successor) will not require a
   re-derivation of the data-side scaling law — the law is
   now a structural fact rather than a vocabulary-conditional
   approximation.
