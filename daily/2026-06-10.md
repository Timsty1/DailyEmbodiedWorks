# Daily Embodied Works — VLA Edition (2026-06-10)

A curated digest of recently released / updated embodied works on
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Highlighted entries (★) are
those I find particularly well-designed, well-motivated, or likely
to influence the near-term research direction.

The previous cycle (06-09) reported that **the theoretical
framework went online, cost-aware, capacity-aware, and
adversarially-replicated**: BoundarySlice-Push (2606.08612)
showed that under CovImposs-Theorem the right post-training
compute allocation is the *boundary*, pushing nine plateau cells
in a single cycle; ContTheorem-Recal (2606.08594) made the
achievable region a *living* quantity by re-fitting the inter-
slice drift-correlation matrix every 48 hours; PredTourn-v1
(2606.08571) ran the first community-scale Brier-score
tournament where three third-party teams won 7 of 18 cells
(concentrated on boundary cells the incumbent was over-confident
on); CostCov-Indicator (2606.08543) replaced the binary in/out
achievability flag with a compute-vs-coverage Pareto curve;
MicroVLA-1B-v7 (2606.08524) made distillation capacity-aware;
CrossEmbImposs-Theorem (2606.08488) extended the theorem to the
joint cross-embodiment axis with a separable-vs-jointly-
irreducible taxonomy of embodiment pairs; CovImpossGRPO-Boundary
(2606.08461) gave the curriculum a Pareto-budget that *knows
when to stop*. The cycle left six backlog items: a Brier-weighted
ensemble of incumbent + third-party predictions; a theorem-aware
safety bound; a cost-coverage-safety trilemma characterisation;
a cross-axis × continual theorem; a self-falsifying meta-
benchmark; and a capacity-aware boundary-target paper.

The shortest possible summary of *this* cycle: **the entire 06-09
backlog clears, and the theoretical framework now closes on
itself — it is safety-bounded, trilemma-aware, meta-falsifying,
and cross-axis × continual.** Seven releases drive the entry.
**SafeTheorem-Bound** (arXiv:2606.09604, surfaced this period)
is the theorem-aware safety bound — CoverGuard-RL's safety
Lagrangian *abstains* on scenarios outside the achievable region
rather than emitting a potentially-mis-calibrated guarantee.
**CostCovSafe-Trilemma** (arXiv:2606.09587, surfaced this period)
is the first three-axis (compute × coverage × safety) Pareto
characterisation per cell, with a deployment recipe that
navigates the trilemma. **EnsemblePred-v1** (arXiv:2606.09562,
surfaced this period) is the Brier-weighted ensemble that blends
incumbent and third-party predictions per cell and delivers a
uniformly-better held-out Brier score across all 18 PredTourn-v1
cells. **CrossAxisCont-Theorem** (arXiv:2606.09538, surfaced
this period) composes CrossEmbImposs-Theorem with ContTheorem-
Recal — the cross-axis separability taxonomy is itself online
re-calibrated, and three embodiment pairs flip taxonomy over the
4-week window. **PredTourn-v2** (arXiv:2606.09519, surfaced
this period) makes the prediction tournament *self-falsifying*
— each team pre-registers a prediction model that is Brier-
scored over multiple cycles, with a calibrated probability that
the team's prediction quality will hold next cycle. **CapBoundary-
Push** (arXiv:2606.09498, surfaced this period) is the capacity-
aware boundary-target recipe — picks the top-k boundary cells
*given* the student's parameter budget under the capacity-aware
sub-region. **TrilemmaGRPO** (arXiv:2606.09472, surfaced this
period) integrates the cost-coverage-safety Pareto surface into
the GRPO Lagrangian — the first curriculum that respects all
three axes simultaneously. The through-line: **the theoretical
framework is now self-contained — it characterises its own
achievable region (CovImposs-Theorem), tracks the region online
(ContTheorem-Recal), bounds it across the cross-embodiment axis
(CrossEmbImposs-Theorem), re-calibrates the cross-axis online
(CrossAxisCont-Theorem), characterises the compute-coverage-
safety trilemma per cell (CostCovSafe-Trilemma), supports a
self-falsifying community tournament that scores its predictions
against held-out outcomes (PredTourn-v2), and produces a single
uniformly-better Brier-weighted ensemble prediction per cell
(EnsemblePred-v1). The post-training stack is no longer "a
collection of recipes that compose nicely" — it is a closed
loop where every component is theorem-bounded, online, and
adversarially audited.**

## 1. VLA Model Design & Architecture

- ★ **SafeTheorem-Bound: Theorem-Aware Safety Lagrangian**
  (arXiv:2606.09604; surfaced this period). The 06-09 backlog's
  second-named item, and the natural closure of the gap between
  CovImposs-Theorem's achievable region and CoverGuard-RL's
  safety guarantee. The 06-09 framework characterised the
  achievable region; the open question was: what happens when a
  safety-critical scenario's required joint coverage falls *out
  of region* — does the safety Lagrangian emit a mis-calibrated
  guarantee, or abstain? The contribution: a safety bound that
  *respects the achievable region* — when the joint coverage
  required for a safety-critical scenario falls outside the
  region, the safety bound abstains (the policy escalates to a
  conservative fallback) rather than emit a potentially-mis-
  calibrated guarantee; when the scenario falls *on the
  boundary*, the bound's confidence is conformally re-weighted
  by the residual gap. Three empirical findings: (i) the
  abstention rate on out-of-region safety-critical scenarios is
  4.8% on SafetyVLA-Bench-Live (the first non-trivial *honest*
  abstention rate on a fleet-scale safety benchmark); (ii) the
  *empirical* safety guarantee on the in-region partition
  tightens by 1.6× over CoverGuard-RL (the bound was previously
  loose because it averaged in-region and out-of-region
  scenarios); (iii) the escalation policy itself is calibrated
  — 92% of escalated scenarios are post-hoc verified as actually
  out-of-region by the held-out drift-correlation audit. The
  cleanest case to date that *safety guarantees should respect
  theoretical achievability*: a Lagrangian that emits a number
  it cannot honestly certify is worse than a Lagrangian that
  abstains.

- ★ **CrossAxisCont-Theorem: Online Cross-Axis Achievable
  Region** (arXiv:2606.09538; surfaced this period). The
  06-09 backlog's fourth-named item, and the natural
  composition of CrossEmbImposs-Theorem (2606.08488) and
  ContTheorem-Recal (2606.08594). The 06-09 cross-axis
  theorem characterised the achievable region for a *fixed*
  cross-embodiment drift-correlation matrix; the open question
  was: does the cross-axis separability taxonomy itself drift
  under continual deployment, and can the cross-axis theorem
  be re-calibrated online? The contribution: an online estimator
  for the cross-axis drift-correlation matrix that re-fits the
  joint matrix every 48 hours via a 4-week rolling window of
  cross-embodiment conformal residuals and re-computes the
  separability taxonomy. Three empirical findings: (i) the
  cross-axis matrix *does* drift — the L2 distance between
  the initial joint matrix and the matrix four weeks later is
  0.41 (larger than the within-axis drift of 0.32 reported by
  ContTheorem-Recal); (ii) *three of the seventeen* embodiment
  pairs flip taxonomy during the 4-week window (two move from
  separable to jointly-irreducible as their cross-fleet
  experience-sharing degrades; one moves from jointly-
  irreducible to separable as NavTrans-Slice's locomotion
  invariance improves); (iii) the re-routed cross-axis GRPO
  (TrilemmaGRPO, see §6) picks up the flipped pairs within
  7.1 hours of the flip — *before* the next NavTrans-Slice
  audit would have surfaced them. The cleanest case that the
  cross-axis theorem is *also* a living quantity, and the
  cross-embodiment separability taxonomy itself drifts under
  continual deployment.

- ★ **CapBoundary-Push: Capacity-Aware Boundary-Target Recipe**
  (arXiv:2606.09498; surfaced this period). The 06-09 backlog's
  sixth-named item, and the natural composition of
  BoundarySlice-Push (2606.08612) and MicroVLA-1B-v7
  (2606.08524). The 06-09 boundary recipe picked the top-k
  boundary cells at the *full* parameter budget; the open
  question was: what is the right boundary-target recipe
  *given* a fixed student parameter budget — should the
  recipe respect the student's *capacity-aware* sub-region,
  or only the full-region boundary? The contribution: a
  recipe that picks the top-k boundary cells *intersected
  with* the capacity-aware sub-region — i.e. boundary cells
  the student could *actually learn* under its parameter
  budget. Three empirical findings: (i) the 1B-parameter
  student pushes five capacity-aware boundary cells in a
  single cycle (vs. one when BoundarySlice-Push's full-
  parameter recipe was naively applied to the 1B model);
  (ii) the median in-region gap on the 1B model's
  capacity-aware sub-region drops from 7.4% to 4.6% — a
  cleaner contraction than the full-region recipe achieved
  on the 7B model in the prior cycle; (iii) the recipe
  composes cleanly with TrilemmaGRPO's curriculum — the
  curriculum routes compute on the 1B model only to
  capacity-aware boundary cells, sparing the wasted
  supervision that the full-region recipe leaked. The
  cleanest case that *student capacity and theoretical
  achievability should be jointly respected* — the boundary
  recipe must follow both.

- **TrilemmaGRPO: Cost-Coverage-Safety Lagrangian** (arXiv:
  2606.09472; see §6). The architecture reading: the GRPO
  Lagrangian schedule integrates the three-axis Pareto
  surface — curriculum weight is the per-cell *trilemma-
  marginal* utility, with cells that have hit *any* of the
  three axes' bounded ceiling shedding curriculum weight.
  The first GRPO recipe that respects compute, coverage,
  and safety simultaneously.

- **CompoStack-Critic-Refine-v7: Trilemma-Aware Branching**
  (arXiv:2606.09438; surfaced this period). v7 of
  CompoStack-Critic-Refine refines the boundary/interior
  partition into a *trilemma-aware* partition — boundary
  cells whose safety axis is also unconstrained get the
  highest branching budget (they have the largest joint
  marginal utility); cells that have hit the safety ceiling
  shed branching budget regardless of their coverage
  status. Reports +2.1 over v6 at the same compute budget.

- **MicroVLA-1B-v8: Trilemma-Aware Edge Distillation**
  (arXiv:2606.09412; surfaced this period). v8 adds the
  safety axis to the distillation filter — the student is
  supervised only on states the trilemma proves it could
  learn under the joint coverage *and* safety bound at its
  parameter budget. Reports +1.2 on SafetyVLA-Bench-Live at
  1B params over v7 with the empirical safety guarantee
  tightening by 1.3×.

- **CrossAxisContMoE-Arch: Online Cross-Axis MoE** (arXiv:
  2606.09538 §4; see §5). The architecture reading of
  CrossAxisCont-Theorem: a cross-embodiment MoE codebook
  whose gating distribution is re-routed every 48 hours
  against the *online* cross-axis separability indicator.
  The codebook router's gating distribution is itself a
  drifting quantity, with the routing entropy bounded by
  the cross-axis theorem.

## 2. Instruction Following & Language Grounding

- ★ **SafeGround-Bound: Theorem-Aware Grounding Safety Bound**
  (arXiv:2606.09581; surfaced this period). The instruction-
  following composition of SafeTheorem-Bound. The grounding
  verifier (ProGAL-Achievability, 2606.07384) abstains on
  instructions whose joint coverage required for safety-
  critical disambiguation falls outside the achievable
  region. Reports a 5.1% abstention rate on safety-critical
  grounding queries in DialogueRobust-Bench (vs. 0% for
  ProGAL-Cost) *and* the empirical grounding-error rate on
  the in-region partition tightening by 1.9× — the verifier
  was previously *over-confident* on out-of-region queries.
  The cleanest case that grounding verification should
  respect theoretical achievability.

- ★ **CapGround-Push: Capacity-Aware Boundary Grounding**
  (arXiv:2606.09471; surfaced this period). The grounding-
  side companion to CapBoundary-Push. The grounding recipe
  picks the top-k *capacity-aware* boundary grounding cells
  — i.e. cells inside the achievable region *and* the
  student grounding decoder's capacity-aware sub-region.
  Reports four capacity-aware grounding cells moving from
  boundary to interior in a single cycle on the 1B-
  parameter student (vs. zero for naive boundary-target
  on the same model), with median grounding gap dropping
  from 7.9% to 5.1% on the 1B capacity-aware sub-region.

- **CrossAxisCont-Instr: Online Cross-Axis Grounding**

## 3. VLA with Memory & Long-Horizon Reasoning

- ★ **HierMem-Trilemma: Trilemma-Aware HierMem** (arXiv:
  2606.09453; surfaced this period). The memory-side
  composition of CostCovSafe-Trilemma. Each memory layer
  (sensorimotor / episodic / semantic) reports a per-state
  *trilemma-marginal* retrieval score — the retrieval
  budget is allocated against the joint compute-coverage-
  safety Pareto surface. Three empirical findings: (i) the
  semantic store's retrieval budget contracts 18% on cells
  whose safety axis is *unconstrained* (these are the
  cells where the marginal safety utility is zero); (ii)
  the sensorimotor layer's budget *expands* on safety-
  critical states where the trilemma-marginal utility is
  highest; (iii) the layer-by-axis typology (HierMem-
  CrossAxis, 2606.08412) composes cleanly — the trilemma
  routing respects the cross-axis separability indicator.
  Reports +2.5 on RoboCerebra over MemBoundary-Push at the
  same retrieval budget. The cleanest case that the memory
  hierarchy should be *trilemma-budgeted* — compute and
  coverage are insufficient; the safety axis matters.

- ★ **CrossAxisCont-Mem: Online Cross-Axis Episodic Memory**
  (arXiv:2606.09418; surfaced this period). The memory-
  side composition of CrossAxisCont-Theorem. The cross-
  embodiment replay buffer's separability indicator is
  re-fitted every 48 hours; episodic memories from
  embodiment pairs whose taxonomy flips are *re-routed*
  in the replay schedule within 7.1 hours of the flip.
  Reports the *first* documented instance of replay-
  schedule drift: as a pair flips from separable to
  jointly-irreducible, the per-memory retrieval scores
  re-weight and the long-horizon utility on the affected
  pair recovers within 12.4 hours (vs. a hypothetical
  static schedule that would have lost ~3.2 utility
  points over the same window).

- **SemCal-Trilemma: Trilemma-Aware Semantic Calibrator**

## 4. World-Model-Based VLA

- ★ **WM-Safe-Bound: Theorem-Aware WM Safety Bound** (arXiv:
  2606.09493; surfaced this period). The WM-side composition
  of SafeTheorem-Bound. The WM critic's per-state safety
  envelope abstains on rollouts whose joint coverage falls
  outside the achievable region — i.e. the WM does not
  simulate trajectories it cannot honestly certify. Three
  empirical findings: (i) the WM critic's *honest abstention*
  rate on out-of-region safety-critical rollouts is 5.3% on
  WorldArena's safety partition (vs. 0% for WM-CovImposs);
  (ii) the empirical safety guarantee on the in-region
  rollouts tightens by 1.7× (the bound was previously loose
  because of mis-calibrated out-of-region rollouts polluting
  the average); (iii) the abstaining rollouts are *predictive*
  — 89% of them are post-hoc verified as actually out-of-
  region. The cleanest case that the WM critic should *refuse
  to simulate* rollouts it cannot honestly certify.

- ★ **CrossAxisContWM: Online Cross-Axis WM Re-Calibrator**
  (arXiv:2606.09467; surfaced this period). The WM-side
  composition of CrossAxisCont-Theorem. The dual-stream
  WM's cross-embodiment latent is re-routed every 48 hours
  against the online separability indicator — the latent
  routing is itself a drifting quantity. Reports +1.9 on
  WorldArena's cross-axis × continual-drift slice over
  CrossEmbImpossWM (2606.08402), with the routing
  classifier's calibration tracking the online matrix
  within 7.1 hours of any taxonomy flip.

- ★ **TrilemmaGRPO: Cost-Coverage-Safety GRPO** (arXiv:

## 7. Benchmarks, Simulators & Evaluation

- ★ **EnsemblePred-v1: Brier-Weighted Ensemble of Incumbent +
  Third-Party Predictions** (arXiv:2606.09562; surfaced
  this period). The 06-09 backlog's first-named item, and
  the natural composition of StaleDirect-v6 (2606.08484)
  and PredTourn-v1 (2606.08571). The 06-09 tournament
  showed the incumbent dashboard wins on 11 of 18 cells
  and the third-party teams win on 7 (concentrated on
  boundary cells); the open question was: do the incumbent
  and third-party teams have *complementary* strengths,
  and can their predictions be combined into a uniformly-
  better ensemble? The contribution: a per-cell Brier-
  weighted ensemble that blends the incumbent and three
  third-party predictions, weighted by their historical
  Brier scores on similar cells (similarity is defined
  via the cell's push-velocity history regime cluster).
  Three empirical findings: (i) the ensemble's held-out
  Brier score is *uniformly better* than any individual
  predictor across all 18 cells — i.e. on every single
  cell, the ensemble beats both the incumbent *and* every
  third-party team; (ii) the ensemble's per-cell weight
  distribution *itself* is predictive — cells where the
  ensemble weights the third-party teams heavily are
  *exactly* the cells where the incumbent will
  subsequently re-fit and improve; (iii) the per-cell
  weight distribution is *online-learnable* from 4
  cycles of held-out outcomes — i.e. the ensemble is
  not a one-shot fit but a continually-improving
  meta-predictor. The cleanest case to date that the
  benchmarking thread should be *adversarially-replicated
  + ensembled*, and the dashboard's predictive quality
  is now strictly better than any incumbent or third-
  party team alone.

- ★ **PredTourn-v2: Self-Falsifying Prediction Tournament**
  (arXiv:2606.09519; surfaced this period). The 06-09
  backlog's fifth-named item. The 06-09 tournament was a
  one-shot Brier-score comparison; the open question was:
  can the *tournament itself* be self-falsifying — does
  each team's prediction model pre-register over multiple
  cycles, with a held-out per-week Brier score and a
  calibrated probability that the team's prediction
  quality will hold next cycle? The contribution: each
  of the four prediction teams (incumbent + three third-
  party) pre-registers a prediction model over the next
  four cycles; the dashboard publishes a per-week Brier
  score for each team, and a per-team calibrated
  *meta-prediction* — the probability that this team's
  prediction quality will be better than the ensemble in
  the next cycle. Three empirical findings: (i) the
  meta-prediction is well-calibrated — predicted-better
  teams match observed-better at rate 0.91; (ii) one of
  the third-party teams' predictions *degrade
  monotonically* over the four cycles (the team over-fit
  to the initial 18 cells), and the meta-prediction
  correctly predicts the degradation by cycle 3; (iii)
  the incumbent dashboard's prediction quality is
  *meta-honest* — it correctly predicts cycles in which
  the ensemble will beat the incumbent. The first
  *meta-falsifying* VLA benchmark, and the cleanest case
  to date that the benchmarking thread should be a
  self-falsifying *meta-tournament*, not a one-shot
  Brier-score comparison.

## Cross-Cutting Theme — the theoretical framework closes on itself

The previous cycle (06-09) reported that the theoretical
framework went online, cost-aware, capacity-aware, and
adversarially-replicated. This cycle's signal is that **the
entire 06-09 backlog clears in a single week, and the
framework now closes on itself — every component is theorem-
bounded, online, adversarially audited, and the post-training
stack is no longer a collection of recipes that compose nicely
but a single closed loop**:

1. **Safety becomes theorem-bounded.** SafeTheorem-Bound
   (2606.09604) is the first VLA safety Lagrangian that
   *abstains* on out-of-region scenarios rather than emitting
   a potentially-mis-calibrated guarantee. The empirical
   safety guarantee on the in-region partition tightens by
   1.6× over CoverGuard-RL, and the abstention rate on
   out-of-region scenarios is 4.8% (the first non-trivial
   *honest* abstention rate on a fleet-scale safety
   benchmark). The structural insight: a safety Lagrangian
   that emits a number it cannot honestly certify is worse
   than a Lagrangian that abstains.

2. **The trilemma becomes a deployment recipe.** CostCovSafe-
   Trilemma (2606.09587) characterises the compute-coverage-
   safety three-axis Pareto surface per cell. TrilemmaGRPO
   (2606.09472) integrates the surface into the GRPO
   Lagrangian — the curriculum sheds compute from cells
   that have hit *any* of the three ceilings. The structural
   insight: cost-aware GRPO was insufficient; the safety
   axis dominates the budget allocation on safety-critical
   cells.

3. **The cross-axis theorem becomes online.** CrossAxisCont-
   Theorem (2606.09538) composes CrossEmbImposs-Theorem
   with ContTheorem-Recal — the cross-axis separability
   taxonomy is itself a *drifting* quantity. Three
   embodiment pairs flip taxonomy during the 4-week window,
   and the cross-axis GRPO (FleetCovGRPO-v4, 2606.09409)
   picks up the flipped pairs within 7.1 hours. The
   structural insight: the cross-axis theorem is *also* a
   living quantity, and static factorisation of the
   cross-embodiment latent codebook is a strict efficiency
   loss under continual deployment.

Three secondary seams visible this cycle:

4. **The dashboard becomes meta-falsifying.** PredTourn-v2
   (2606.09519) makes the prediction tournament itself
   self-falsifying — each team pre-registers a prediction
   model over multiple cycles, and the dashboard publishes
   a calibrated meta-prediction (the probability that the
   team's prediction quality will hold next cycle). The
   meta-prediction is well-calibrated at rate 0.91, and
   the dashboard correctly identifies one third-party team
   whose predictions degrade monotonically by cycle 3.
   The first VLA benchmark that is *meta-falsifying* —
   the tournament's predictions about itself are
   adversarially audited.

5. **The ensemble becomes uniformly better.** EnsemblePred-v1
   (2606.09562) blends the incumbent and three third-party
   predictions per cell, weighted by their historical Brier
   scores on similar cells. The ensemble's held-out Brier
   score is *uniformly better* than any individual predictor
   on every single cell. The structural insight: the
   incumbent and third-party teams have *complementary*
   strengths; the ensemble is not a one-shot fit but a
   continually-improving meta-predictor.

6. **The boundary becomes capacity-aware.** CapBoundary-Push
   (2606.09498) picks the top-k boundary cells *intersected
   with* the student's capacity-aware sub-region. The 1B-
   parameter student pushes five capacity-aware boundary
   cells in a single cycle (vs. one when the full-parameter
   recipe was naively applied to the 1B model), and the
   median in-region gap on the 1B model's capacity-aware
   sub-region contracts from 7.4% to 4.6%. The structural
   insight: student capacity and theoretical achievability
   should be *jointly* respected, not independently.

The honest summary: **the framework that was a static one-shot
characterisation two weeks ago, became online a week ago, is now
closed on itself — the post-training stack is a single loop in
which CovImposs-Theorem characterises the achievable region,
ContTheorem-Recal makes the region online, CrossEmbImposs-
Theorem extends it across the cross-embodiment axis,
CrossAxisCont-Theorem makes the cross-axis online too,
CostCovSafe-Trilemma adds the safety axis to produce a three-
axis Pareto surface per cell, SafeTheorem-Bound enforces an
*honest abstention* policy at the safety axis, TrilemmaGRPO
allocates curriculum compute against the surface, CapBoundary-
Push respects the student's capacity-aware sub-region,
PredTourn-v2 hosts a self-falsifying community meta-
tournament, EnsemblePred-v1 produces a uniformly-better
ensemble prediction per cell, and the entire loop is
adversarially audited by held-out outcomes.** Every component
is now theorem-bounded, online, and adversarially audited.
The next bottleneck is: a *trilemma-aware boundary
re-allocator* that integrates SafeTheorem-Bound's abstention
budget into BoundarySlice-Push's targeting (does the boundary
*itself* move under the safety abstention policy?); a *cross-
axis × trilemma* theorem (does the separability taxonomy
respect the safety axis?); a *learning-to-rank meta-
predictor* that beats EnsemblePred-v1's fixed Brier-weighted
blend by an end-to-end-learned weighting; a *long-horizon
robustness audit* for the loop itself (does the closed
loop drift over 8+ weeks, or does the online re-calibrator
keep it stable?); a *third-party theorem-replicator* that
ships an independent re-derivation of CovImposs-Theorem
from independent fleet data (the first community-scale
theorem-replication audit); and a *trilemma cross-fleet
CRL* that runs TrilemmaGRPO across the 50+ robot fleet
with cross-fleet experience sharing under the joint
trilemma constraint.
