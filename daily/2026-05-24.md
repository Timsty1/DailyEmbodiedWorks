# Daily Embodied Works — VLA Edition (2026-05-24)

A curated digest of recently released / updated embodied works on
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Highlighted entries (★) are
those I find particularly well-designed, well-motivated, or likely
to influence the near-term research direction.

This cycle is a **grounded roundup**: rather than tracking a single
synthesized storyline, the entries below are pinned to actual arXiv
releases that surfaced this period, with the freshest items being
**ALAM** (2605.10819, latent action) and the **World Model for Robot
Learning** survey (2605.00080). The dominant signal across the intake
is convergence on three structural ideas the field had been circling:
(1) *latent actions are becoming algebraically/structurally
constrained* rather than free-form codebooks (ALAM, JALA, UniLACT);
(2) *world models are graduating from prediction to RL substrate* —
several teams now train the policy by reinforcement *inside* a learned
video/dynamics model (GigaBrain, World-Gymnast, RISE); and (3) the
community is finally treating *"the policy ignores the instruction"*
as a first-class, measurable failure rather than an anecdote (When
Vision Overrides Language, CAST, train-free recalibration). The
memory thread also matured: belief-state recursion (RB-VLA) and
static/dynamic token disentanglement (SD-VLA, which ships its own
LIBERO-memory benchmark) are the two cleanest new takes.

---

## 1. VLA Model Design & Architecture

- ★ **MMaDA-VLA: Large Diffusion VLA with Unified Multimodal
  Instruction and Generation** (arXiv:2603.25406). Represents
  language, images, *and* continuous robot controls in a single
  discrete token space and trains one backbone with a masked-token
  denoising objective that jointly generates a future goal
  observation **and** an action chunk in parallel. The motivation is
  clean: a discrete-diffusion VLM and an action decoder need not be
  two separate stacks if both are token-denoising problems. Reports
  **98.0%** average LIBERO success and **4.78** average length on
  CALVIN — SOTA-class on both. The "predict the goal frame and the
  action chunk in the same denoising pass" framing is the part worth
  borrowing; it folds a lightweight world-model objective into the
  architecture for free.

- ★ **Evo-1: Lightweight VLA with Preserved Semantic Alignment**
  (arXiv:2511.04555). Targets the recurring failure where action
  fine-tuning silently destroys the VLM's pretrained vision-language
  alignment. Evo-1 keeps the semantic alignment intact while adding a
  compact action pathway, hitting strong manipulation numbers at a
  fraction of the parameter budget. The right efficiency target — the
  field keeps shrinking backbones but Evo-1's point is that *what you
  preserve* matters more than *how small you go*.

- **VITA-VLA: Teaching VLMs to Act via Action-Expert Distillation**
  (arXiv:2510.09607). Two-stage recipe that equips a pretrained VLM
  with action generation by distilling from a small, already-trained
  action model — cheaper than learning the action head from scratch
  and avoids perturbing the VLM. A pragmatic distillation baseline for
  compute-constrained groups.

- **SD-VLA: Efficient Long-Horizon VLA via Static–Dynamic
  Disentanglement** (arXiv:2602.03983). Splits visual input into
  multi-level *static* and *dynamic* tokens, keeps a single copy of
  the static tokens across frames, and reuses the KV cache — cutting
  context length for multi-frame integration. Also see §3: it ships
  the **LIBERO-memory** benchmark. A genuinely useful efficiency idea
  grounded in the observation that most of a trajectory's pixels do
  not change.

- *(Carried efficiency anchors: TinyVLA, SmolVLA, π₀ / π₀-FAST,
  OpenVLA-OFT; surveys: Efficient VLA 2510.24795, 2510.17111.)*

## 2. Instruction Following & Language Grounding

- ★ **When Vision Overrides Language: Evaluating and Mitigating
  Counterfactual Failures in VLAs** (arXiv:2602.17659). The sharpest
  diagnostic of the cycle. Shows that VLAs trained on goal-driven
  datasets routinely learn a *vision-only* policy and merely simulate
  instruction following — an "illusion of instruction following" that
  collapses precisely when vision and language disagree
  (counterfactual prompts). It both *measures* the gap and proposes a
  mitigation. This reframes a vague complaint ("VLAs don't really
  listen") into a quantified, reproducible failure mode, which is what
  the subfield needed.

- ★ **CAST: Counterfactual Labels Improve Instruction Following**
  (arXiv:2508.13446). The constructive complement to the paper above:
  use a VLM to relabel existing robot demonstrations with diverse,
  counterfactual language, increasing the granularity and diversity of
  the grounding signal so the policy can no longer shortcut through
  vision. Cheap, data-centric, and directly addresses the root cause
  rather than the symptom.

- **Restoring Linguistic Grounding via Train-Free Attention
  Recalibration** (arXiv:2603.06001). Recovers eroded language
  grounding *without retraining* by recalibrating attention at
  inference — a zero-cost intervention for already-deployed
  checkpoints. Pairs naturally with the two papers above as the
  "no-budget" option.

- **Actions as Language: Fine-Tuning VLMs into VLAs Without
  Catastrophic Forgetting** (arXiv:2509.22195). Argues that treating
  actions as just another language modality preserves the VLM's core
  capabilities, enabling zero-shot generalization to novel,
  open-world-semantic and *multilingual* instructions. The
  catastrophic-forgetting framing connects directly to Evo-1 (§1).

- **LangForce: Bayesian Decomposition of VLAs via Latent Action
  Queries** (arXiv:2601.15197) and **Point What You Mean: Visually
  Grounded Instruction Policy** (arXiv:2512.18933). Two further takes
  on tightening the language→action binding — the former via a
  probabilistic decomposition over latent action queries, the latter
  via explicit pointing/grounding of the referenced object.

## 3. VLA with Memory & Long-Horizon Reasoning

- ★ **RB-VLA: Recursive Belief Vision-Language-Action Models**
  (arXiv:2602.20659). Maintains a compact recursive *belief state* —
  a latent that encodes task-relevant history, dynamics, and object
  interactions — instead of stuffing raw history into context. Reports
  **+52.5%** and **+37.5%** higher success on multi-stage pick-and-place
  and stacking respectively over prior VLAs. The belief-state framing
  is the right abstraction for partial observability: it is the
  POMDP-correct answer to "what should a VLA remember," and it scales
  better than naive context growth.

- ★ **SD-VLA + the LIBERO-memory benchmark** (arXiv:2602.03983; also
  §1). Beyond the efficiency contribution, SD-VLA introduces
  **LIBERO-memory**, an episodic-memory-inspired split that explicitly
  scores memory-dependent, long-horizon tasks — addressing the gap
  that standard LIBERO is essentially memoryless. A benchmark that
  comes packaged with the method that motivates it.

- **TempoFit: Plug-and-Play Layer-Wise Temporal KV Memory**
  (arXiv:2603.07647). Attacks the fact that mainstream VLAs infer in a
  *memoryless*, single-frame, implicitly-Markovian way even though
  real operation is partially observable. Adds a per-layer temporal KV
  memory that drops into existing backbones without retraining the
  policy. The "plug-and-play" framing makes it the low-friction way to
  retrofit memory onto a deployed VLA.

- *(Carried memory primaries: MemoryVLA — Cognition-Memory-Action,
  84.0% on 12 real tasks, +26 on long-horizon (2508.19236); MAP-VLA —
  retrieval over a demonstration memory library, up to +7% sim /
  +25% real (2511.09516); MemER (2510.20328); Long-VLA (2508.19958).)*

## 4. World-Model-Based VLA

- ★ **GigaBrain-0.5M*: a VLA That Learns From World-Model-Based RL**
  (arXiv:2602.12099). Trains the policy by reinforcement learning
  *inside* a learned world model via **RAMP** (RL via world
  Model-conditioned Policy), reporting **~30%** gains on genuinely hard
  long-horizon tasks — Laundry Folding, Box Packing, Espresso
  Preparation. The significance is that the world model is no longer a
  passive predictor; it is the environment the policy is optimized
  against, which is the cleanest path to data-efficient improvement on
  contact-rich, long-horizon skills.

- ★ **World Model for Robot Learning: A Comprehensive Survey**
  (arXiv:2605.00080). The freshest synthesis of the
  world-model-for-robotics literature, and a useful map precisely
  because the subfield is fragmenting fast (prediction models, latent
  dynamics, action-conditioned video generators, RL substrates). Read
  it to place GigaBrain / World-Gymnast / RISE on a single axis.

- **World-Gymnast: Training Robots with RL in a World Model**
  (arXiv:2602.02454). RL fine-tuning of a VLA where the "simulator" is
  an action-conditioned video generator and the reward is computed
  *from the predicted frames*. A concrete instantiation of "reward
  from imagined video," and a natural pairing with GigaBrain's RAMP.

- **RISE: Self-Improving Robot Policy with Compositional World Model**
  (arXiv:2602.11075). Couples a *compositional* world model with a
  self-improvement loop, so the policy bootstraps from its own
  model-generated experience. The compositional structure is the
  interesting bet — it targets the generalization the monolithic
  world models lack.

- **VLA-JEPA: Enhancing VLAs with a Latent World Model**
  (arXiv:2602.10098). A deliberately *simple* two-stage recipe — JEPA
  pretraining, then action-head fine-tuning — that sidesteps the
  multi-stage complexity of prior latent-action / world-model
  pipelines. The minimalism is the point; it is the easy-to-reproduce
  baseline the world-model-VLA line needed.

- **Do World-Action Models Generalize Better than VLAs? A Robustness
  Study** (arXiv:2603.22078). A timely robustness audit comparing
  world-action models against standard VLAs (and engaging SimpleVLA-RL
  scaling). The kind of skeptical, comparative study that keeps the
  world-model hype honest.

## 5. Latent Action Embeddings / Action Representation

- ★ **ALAM: Algebraically Consistent Latent Action Model**
  (arXiv:2605.10819, May 11, 2026). The standout fresh release.
  Couples *structured* latent transitions (with algebraic consistency
  constraints on how latent actions compose) to a flow-based policy
  generator. Transferred to VLA policies it lifts average success from
  **47.9% → 85.0%** on MetaWorld MT50 and **94.1% → 98.1%** on LIBERO,
  with consistent real-world gains. The motivation is exactly right:
  free-form latent-action codebooks have no reason to *compose*
  sensibly, and enforcing algebraic structure on the latent transition
  is what lets the representation transfer and chain. Best-motivated
  latent-action paper in a while.

- ★ **JALA: Joint-Aligned Latent Action — Scalable VLA Pretraining in
  the Wild** (arXiv:2602.21736). Scales latent-action pretraining to a
  **7.5M-clip / >2,000-hour** corpus blending lab and in-the-wild
  footage, with a joint-alignment objective that yields more realistic
  hand motion and clear downstream manipulation gains. The data-scale
  result that the latent-action-pretraining line (LAPA lineage) needed
  to prove the recipe survives messy in-the-wild video.

- **UniLACT: Depth-Aware RGB Latent Action Learning**
  (arXiv:2602.20231). Injects geometric structure via depth-aware
  latent pretraining so downstream policies inherit stronger spatial
  priors. The depth conditioning is a sensible, low-cost way to make
  latent actions 3D-aware without a depth sensor at deployment.

- **Hierarchical Latent Action Model** (arXiv:2603.05815, ICLR 2026
  workshop). A hierarchical decomposition of latent actions
  (coarse intent over fine modulation) — the structural complement to
  ALAM's algebraic constraint; the two ideas (hierarchy + algebraic
  composition) are the obvious next combination.

- *(Carried anchors: LAPA / Latent Action Pretraining from Videos
  (2410.11758), Latent Action Pretraining Through World Modeling
  (2509.18428), UniVLA / task-centric latent actions (2505.06111).)*

## 6. Reinforcement Learning for VLA

- ★ **GigaBrain-0.5M* / RAMP** (see §4) — the cycle's headline RL
  result: reinforcement learning of the VLA *inside* a learned world
  model, ~30% on the hardest long-horizon tasks. The world-model-as-
  RL-substrate framing is where the RL-for-VLA line is converging.

- **VLA-RL: Masterful and General Robotic Manipulation with Scalable
  RL** (arXiv:2505.18719). The scalable-RL-for-VLA reference: treats
  manipulation as a scalable RL problem on top of a VLA backbone.
  Still the baseline the world-model-RL papers (§4) measure against,
  and the substrate the robustness study (2603.22078) probes.

- **World-Gymnast** (arXiv:2602.02454; also §4). The
  reward-from-imagined-video instantiation of in-world-model RL —
  belongs equally to this section and §4.

- *(Carried RL anchors: SimpleVLA-RL / PRIME-RL (GitHub), the
  scalable-RL recipes that the §4 world-model methods build their
  policy-gradient updates on.)*

## 7. Benchmarks, Simulators & Evaluation

- ★ **REALM: A Real-to-Sim Validated Benchmark for Generalization in
  Robotic Manipulation** (arXiv:2512.19562). A simulation benchmark
  built specifically to *establish correlation between simulated and
  real-world performance* — the missing piece in a field where sim
  scores routinely fail to predict real success. The real-to-sim
  validation is the methodological contribution; if it holds up, it is
  the right way to make cheap sim evaluation trustworthy.

- ★ **Rethinking the Practicality of VLAs: A Comprehensive Benchmark
  and an Improved Baseline** (arXiv:2602.22663). Pushes back on the
  benchmark fragmentation problem directly — a unified, practicality-
  oriented benchmark plus a strengthened baseline. Useful precisely
  because "everyone reports on their own tasks and metrics" remains the
  field's biggest comparability problem.

- **Embodied World-Model Evaluation Turing Test (WoW / WoW-World-Eval)**
  (arXiv:2601.04137). A standardized framework for evaluating embodied
  *world models* — the evaluation substrate the §4 surge needs, given
  how many world-model VLAs now exist with no common yardstick.

- **AGIBOT Genie Sim 3.0** (industry, simulation platform). Adds
  GenieSim-Manip (atomic-skill execution + long-horizon composition),
  robustness testing under real-world disturbances, and explicit
  Sim2Real evaluation tasks. Notable as an industry simulator now
  shipping a structured evaluation suite, not just an environment.

- *(Carried benchmark anchors: LIBERO, CALVIN, MetaWorld MT50,
  RoboCasa, SAPIEN / PartNet-Mobility, plus the LIBERO-memory split
  from SD-VLA (§3).)*

## 8. Applications: Navigation, Aerial, Dexterous & Humanoid

- ★ **METIS: Multi-Source Egocentric Training for Integrated Dexterous
  VLA** (arXiv:2511.17366). Trains a dexterous VLA from *multi-source
  egocentric* data (human video + robot), targeting the integrated
  whole-hand dexterity that single-source datasets cannot supply. The
  egocentric-data leverage is the well-motivated part — dexterous data
  is scarce, and human egocentric video is the most abundant source of
  hand-object interaction.

- **ABot-N0: VLA Foundation Model for Versatile Embodied Navigation**
  (arXiv:2602.11598). A foundation-model technical report aimed at
  *navigation* rather than tabletop manipulation — a useful reminder
  that the VLA recipe is migrating into mobility, where the action
  space and horizon differ substantially.

- **AIR-VLA: VLA Systems for Aerial Manipulation** (arXiv:2601.21602).
  Extends the VLA stack to aerial manipulation, where the platform
  itself is non-stationary — a genuinely harder control regime that
  stress-tests the perception/action loop.

- *(Carried application anchors: DexVLA (2502.05855), Bi-VLA bimanual
  (2405.06039), humanoid + driving deployments tracked in prior
  cycles.)*

## Cross-Cutting Theme — the three quiet convergences

Stepping back from any single paper, three structural shifts are
visible across this cycle's real intake:

1. **Latent actions are acquiring structure.** ALAM (algebraic
   composition), the hierarchical latent action model, and UniLACT
   (depth/geometry) all reject the free-form codebook in favor of
   *constrained* latent representations. The motivating intuition is
   shared: a latent action vocabulary that does not compose, chain, or
   respect geometry will not transfer. ALAM's MetaWorld jump
   (47.9 → 85.0%) is the strongest single piece of evidence that the
   constraint, not the capacity, is what was missing.

2. **World models are becoming the RL environment, not a side
   predictor.** GigaBrain (RAMP), World-Gymnast (reward-from-imagined-
   video), and RISE (compositional self-improvement) all train the
   policy *against* the model. The new survey (2605.00080) and the WoW
   evaluation Turing test (2601.04137) arriving in the same window is
   not a coincidence — the subfield is large enough to need both a map
   and a referee.

3. **"The policy doesn't actually listen" is now a measured problem.**
   When-Vision-Overrides-Language names the "illusion of instruction
   following," CAST supplies the counterfactual-relabeling fix, and the
   train-free recalibration paper offers the no-budget patch. Together
   they convert a long-standing anecdote into a diagnose → fix →
   retrofit pipeline.

The natural next combinations write themselves: **ALAM's algebraic
latent actions × a world-model RL substrate** (structured actions are
exactly what make imagined rollouts compose), and **RB-VLA's belief
state × counterfactual instruction grounding** (a belief state is the
right place to represent "what the instruction actually asked for"
separately from "what vision suggests").

## Picks of the day (if you only read three)

1. **ALAM (2605.10819)** — the freshest release and the best-motivated
   latent-action paper in a while. Enforcing *algebraic consistency* on
   latent-action composition lifts MetaWorld MT50 from 47.9% to 85.0%;
   the lesson is that structure, not codebook size, is the bottleneck.

2. **GigaBrain-0.5M* (2602.12099)** — the cleanest demonstration that a
   world model should be the RL *environment*, not a passive predictor.
   ~30% gains on laundry folding / box packing / espresso are exactly
   the contact-rich long-horizon tasks where on-robot RL is too
   expensive. Read alongside the World-Model survey (2605.00080).

3. **When Vision Overrides Language (2602.17659)** — the diagnostic
   that reframes "VLAs ignore instructions" as a measurable
   counterfactual failure. If you train or evaluate a VLA, this should
   change how you build your eval set; pair it with CAST (2508.13446)
   for the fix.

## Open backlog for the next cycle (to watch)

- **Structured-latent-action × world-model RL** — ALAM-style
  algebraically-consistent latent actions used as the action space for
  GigaBrain-style in-world-model RL. Structured actions are precisely
  what should make imagined multi-step rollouts compose without drift.
- **A belief-state policy with explicit instruction grounding** —
  RB-VLA's recursive belief is the natural home for separating
  "instructed goal" from "visual prior," directly attacking the
  counterfactual-failure mode (When-Vision-Overrides-Language).
- **A unified, real-to-sim-validated leaderboard** — REALM
  (2512.19562) and the practicality benchmark (2602.22663) both push on
  comparability; the field still lacks one harness that is
  sim-cheap *and* real-correlated. The pieces now exist to build it.
- **Memory benchmarks beyond LIBERO-memory** — SD-VLA's LIBERO-memory
  and RB-VLA's multi-stage tasks are a start, but an hour-scale,
  streaming, partially-observable memory benchmark remains the obvious
  gap for the RB-VLA / TempoFit / MemoryVLA line to be compared on.
