# Daily Embodied Works — VLA Edition (2026-06-02)

A curated digest of recently released / updated embodied works on
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Highlighted entries (★) are
those I find particularly well-designed, well-motivated, or likely
to influence the near-term research direction.

The previous cycle (06-01) named the **post-training stack** as
*assembled*: World2Act (objective), PLD (data generation), CRL-VLA
(theory), composing over Qwen-VLA / AtomVLA / D-VLA / FPO /
World-Env. The shortest possible summary of *this* cycle: **the
first composition results land, and they reorder the priority of
the components.** Three releases drive the entry. **CompoStack-VLA**
(arXiv:2606.02118, surfaced this period) is the first paper to
*compose* the named components end-to-end and report VLA-Arena
numbers across the factorial grid — and the finding is sharp: the
data-side contribution (PLD-style residual RL) is the *largest
single lever*; the objective-side contribution (World2Act-style
contrastive latent alignment) is the *largest compounding lever*;
the substrate (D-VLA) and update rule (FPO) are *necessary but
saturating*. **SafeVLA-Bound** (arXiv:2606.01744, surfaced this
period) is the safety-side counterpart CRL-VLA was missing: a
*constraint-aware* extension of the goal-conditioned advantage
bound, with the safety margin entering the bound as a *separable
penalty term* that doesn't couple to the stability-plasticity
trade-off. **VLA-Arena-Live** (arXiv:2606.00937, surfaced this
period) closes the evaluation loop: the same factorial difficulty
grid as VLA-Arena, but with *continually-refreshed* task instances
and a *held-out* evaluation pool that resists overfitting to the
benchmark. The through-line: **the stack survives its first
composition pass; the next bottleneck is evaluation that doesn't
go stale and safety bounds that compose with the existing
performance bound.**

## 1. VLA Model Design & Architecture

- ★ **CompoStack-VLA: End-to-End Composition of the Post-Training
  Stack** (arXiv:2606.02118; surfaced this period). The first paper
  to actually compose the named components — Qwen-VLA backbone +
  AtomVLA predictive latent + World2Act contrastive alignment + PLD
  residual-RL data + FPO update rule + D-VLA distributed substrate
  — and report VLA-Arena numbers across all three difficulty levels
  on the factorial grid. The headline result: 78.4% / 64.1% / 41.7%
  success at Easy / Medium / Hard, against 71.2% / 52.8% / 28.3%
  for the strongest single-component baseline (Qwen-VLA + AtomVLA
  alone). More importantly, the ablation table is the first
  empirically-grounded ranking of the post-training stack: PLD
  contributes +6.8 average; World2Act contributes +4.9 but
  *compounds* (+8.3 when PLD is also on); FPO + D-VLA contribute
  +2.1 and saturate quickly. The right composition paper for the
  cycle and the one that should set the post-training recipe going
  forward.

- ★ **Latent-Action MoE-VLA: Mixture-of-Experts over Latent Action
  Codebooks** (arXiv:2606.01985; surfaced this period). The
  cleanest architectural answer to MoLA's "mixture of IDMs" framing
  — instead of mixing the *inverse-dynamics models* (which is
  expensive and hard to load-balance), Latent-Action MoE-VLA mixes
  the *latent action codebooks* themselves, with a learned router
  conditioned on instruction + scene. Two consequences: (i) the
  codebooks specialize — a "rotational" expert, a "translation"
  expert, a "contact-rich" expert emerge without supervision; (ii)
  serving cost stays sub-linear in the number of experts because
  only one codebook is decoded per step. The natural successor to
  MoLA (2605.12167) and the right architectural pair with RotVLA
  (2605.13403) — RotVLA structures the output manifold, MoE-VLA
  structures the *codebook* allocation across that manifold.

- **MicroVLA-1B: 1B-Parameter Generalist VLA via Aggressive Latent
  Compression** (arXiv:2606.01432; surfaced this period). The
  efficiency-frontier paper for the cycle. The contribution is a
  3-stage latent compression — visual tokens → action-relevant
  tokens → action latent — with the compression ratios tuned on
  reconstruction-vs-control. 1B params, 31 ms inference on a single
  RTX 4090, 88.9% LIBERO. The right efficiency contribution at the
  same altitude as TinyVLA / SmolVLA — but with the compression
  *aligned to the action objective* rather than to the visual
  reconstruction loss.

- **DeepThink-VLA: Test-Time Reasoning Allocation for VLA**
  (arXiv:2606.00712; surfaced this period). The right test-time
  scaling contribution. Where AC²-VLA (2601.19634) adapts compute
  per chunk based on novelty, DeepThink-VLA adapts compute per
  *reasoning depth* — the policy chooses how many chain-of-thought
  steps to spend before emitting an action, with the depth governed
  by a learned reasoner-confidence head. The natural pair with
  MoTVLA (2510.18337) on the fast/slow side and the cleanest
  framing of how to spend test-time compute *inside* a single VLA
  weight set.

- **DAM-VLA-v2: Dynamic Action Model + Cross-Embodiment Adapters**
  (arXiv:2606.00528; surfaced this period). DAM-VLA's diffusion-as-
  dynamic-component idea extended with per-embodiment adapter
  modules that don't interfere with the shared VLM trunk. The right
  efficiency answer for the cross-embodiment regime that Embodied4C
  (2512.18028) measures — keep the backbone shared, swap only the
  action adapter.

## 2. Instruction Following & Language Grounding

- ★ **ContextVLA: Conversational Multi-Turn Instruction Following
  for VLAs** (arXiv:2606.01876; surfaced this period). The right
  multi-turn extension of the instruction-following line.
  ContextVLA's contribution is a *dialogue-state-tracking* head
  that maintains a structured representation of the conversation
  across turns ("first the red block, then the blue one on top of
  it"), with the policy conditioned on the *current* turn's intent
  resolved against the cumulative state. The natural complement to
  LIBERO-Para (2603.28301) — LIBERO-Para tests paraphrase
  robustness within a single instruction; ContextVLA tests
  *referential* robustness across multiple instructions. The right
  framing for deployment with a non-expert user who issues
  step-by-step instructions rather than self-contained ones.

- ★ **ProGAL-VLA-v2: Programmatic Goal Alignment with Symbolic
  Verifier** (arXiv:2606.01205; surfaced this period). The ICLR
  2026 follow-up to ProGAL-VLA (2604.09824). The contribution: an
  *executable* symbolic verifier that decomposes the natural-language
  goal into a typed program over scene entities and runs it against
  perception to check sub-goal satisfaction *online*. The verifier
  failure signal feeds back as a dense reward shaping term. The
  right structural answer to "linguistic grounding *and* online
  verification in one pass" — and the cleanest sibling of
  Point-What-You-Mean (2512.18933) on the externalized-grounding
  axis.

- **ActiveVLA-3D: Active Perception + 3D Foresight**
  (arXiv:2606.00489; surfaced this period). The 3D extension of
  ActiveVLA (2601.08325). Instead of just choosing *where to look*,
  the policy generates a 3D foresight (a sparse next-frame point
  cloud) and evaluates the foresight's information gain *before*
  acquiring the next view. The right efficiency move when active
  perception is otherwise dominated by exploration cost.

- **Point-What-You-Mean** (arXiv:2512.18933; carried highlight).
  Still the cycle's cleanest external-grounding contribution; the
  natural complement to ContextVLA at the per-turn level.

- **VLA-Forget** (arXiv:2604.03956; carried highlight). Linguistic
  blindness as a grounding failure.

- **Tactile-VLA** (ICLR 2026; carried). Tactile as a first-class
  modality fused at every layer.

- **LIBERO-Para** (arXiv:2603.28301; carried highlight). Paraphrase
  robustness yardstick.

- **GesVLA** (arXiv:2605.22812; carried highlight). Non-language
  modality grounding.

- **Grounding via Explicit Language-Action Alignment**
  (arXiv:2604.05614; carried). Contrastive trajectory ranking.

- **Clutter-Robust VLAs** (arXiv:2512.22519; carried).
  Object-centric + geometry grounding.

## 3. VLA with Memory & Long-Horizon Reasoning

- ★ **HierMem-VLA: Hierarchical Memory for Day-Long Embodied
  Tasks** (arXiv:2606.01617; surfaced this period). The right
  long-horizon contribution at a horizon longer than anything the
  field has measured to date. HierMem-VLA structures memory as
  three explicit layers: (i) a sub-second sensorimotor buffer
  (proprioception trace), (ii) a minute-scale episodic store
  (keyframe + caption pairs), (iii) a *day-scale* semantic store
  (entity-state graph updated on event detection). The empirical
  finding is sharp: on a new "DayHorizon-50" benchmark with 6–8
  hour kitchen / household tasks, MEM (2603.03596) and EchoVLA
  (2511.18112) collapse beyond the 90-minute mark; HierMem-VLA
  degrades gracefully via the semantic store. The natural pair with
  SD-VLA-2 (2602.03983) on the *efficiency* side — HierMem-VLA
  organizes what's kept; SD-VLA-2 organizes the KV-cache of what's
  shown.

- **EpisodicVLA-Replay: Replay-Buffer-Based Episodic Memory**
  (arXiv:2606.00854; surfaced this period). The replay-side
  contribution to the long-horizon memory line. Where MemoryVLA
  / MAP-VLA *retrieve* from memory, EpisodicVLA-Replay *trains
  with* the memory — recent episodes are sampled into the
  fine-tuning batch with a relevance score conditioned on the
  current task. The natural pair with HierMem-VLA's semantic store
  on the *training* side.

## 4. World-Model-Based VLA

- ★ **WM-Critic-VLA: World Model as a Per-Step Critic**
  (arXiv:2606.02055; surfaced this period). The cleanest framing
  of "what is the world model *for* in a deployed VLA?" yet. The
  observation: World2Act's contrastive alignment is a *training-time*
  use; World-Env's rollouts are a *training-time* use; nothing yet
  uses the WM *at deployment*. WM-Critic-VLA does — at inference,
  the WM evaluates each candidate action by predicting one step
  ahead and scoring whether the resulting latent is *consistent
  with the goal-conditioning channel*, rejecting actions that
  predict drift. The empirical claim: +5.3% on VLA-Arena Hard
  without any policy retraining. The right complement to FPO
  (2510.09976) — FPO uses the WM during fine-tuning; WM-Critic-VLA
  uses it during execution.

- ★ **DUST-Compose: Dual-Stream Diffusion + World-Model Alignment**
  (arXiv:2606.01443; surfaced this period). The composition of
  DUST's dual-stream diffusion (2510.27607) with World2Act's
  contrastive alignment objective. The motivation: DUST solves
  modality collapse; World2Act solves the post-training objective;
  the question is whether they compose without one channel
  dominating the other. Empirically: they do, with a +3.1
  improvement on the WorldArena functional utility score over
  either alone. The cleanest evidence that the two flow-matching
  contributions of the last quarter are *additive*, not
  substitutive.

- **WMRollout-VLA: Imagined Rollout as a Curriculum Generator**
  (arXiv:2606.00691; surfaced this period). The "WM as
  *curriculum*" framing. Where World-Env (2509.24948) uses
  WM-imagined rollouts as a *training environment*, WMRollout-VLA
  uses them to *score the difficulty* of upcoming real-world tasks
  and schedule the curriculum accordingly. The natural pair with
  PLD's residual probe head — both find the brittle states; one
  via real rollouts (PLD), the other via imagined ones
  (WMRollout-VLA).

## 5. Latent Action Embeddings / Action Representation

- ★ **Latent-Action MoE-VLA** (arXiv:2606.01985; see §1). The
  representation-side reading: the contribution is a *codebook
  factorization* — the latent action space is structured into
  rotational / translational / contact-rich sub-codebooks, with
  unsupervised specialization. Composes with RotVLA's manifold
  structure (output side) and World2Act's contrastive objective
  (training side) to give the cycle's most structured latent
  action stack: RotVLA fixes the manifold, World2Act fixes the
  objective, MoE-VLA fixes the codebook routing.

- **LARY-2: Generalizable Latent Action Benchmark, Cross-Embodiment
  Extension** (arXiv:2606.01122; surfaced this period). The
  cross-embodiment extension of LARY (2604.11689). Adds 4
  additional embodiments and a "transfer score" measuring how well
  a latent action codebook trained on one embodiment serves
  another. The right measurement counterpart for the
  cross-embodiment regime — particularly relevant given DAM-VLA-v2's
  adapter design.

## 6. Reinforcement Learning for VLA

- ★ **SafeVLA-Bound: Constraint-Aware Continual VLA Performance
  Bound** (arXiv:2606.01744; surfaced this period). The safety-side
  follow-up CRL-VLA was missing. SafeVLA-Bound extends the
  goal-conditioned advantage-magnitude bound of CRL-VLA
  (2602.03445) with a *safety margin* term that enters as a
  *separable penalty* — i.e. safety violations do not couple to the
  stability-plasticity trade-off, and the bound holds component-wise.
  The empirical companion: a Lagrangian post-training variant of
  PLD that respects the safety constraint without losing the
  data-side gains. The right theoretical answer to "can continual
  RL fine-tuning be safe?" — yes, with the bound made explicit.

- ★ **CompoStack-VLA** (arXiv:2606.02118; see §1). The RL-side
  reading: the first empirical ranking of post-training RL
  components on a factorial benchmark. PLD (data) is the largest
  single lever; World2Act (objective) compounds; FPO (update) and
  D-VLA (substrate) saturate. The composition cost is real but
  manageable — the cycle's most important *empirical* finding.

- ★ **GRPO-VLA-v2: Robust GRPO with Action-Chunked Advantages**
  (arXiv:2606.00982; surfaced this period). The right successor to
  AtomVLA's GRPO setup (2603.08519). The contribution: action-chunked
  group-relative advantages that don't degrade under long-horizon
  reward sparsity, with a learned baseline estimator that handles
  multimodal action distributions. Empirically: matches AtomVLA's
  97.0% LIBERO while gaining +4.1% on the LIBERO-PRO long-horizon
  suite. The cleanest GRPO-side contribution since the original
  AtomVLA.

- **VLA-PPO-Chunked** (arXiv:2509.25718; carried). Action-chunked

## 7. Benchmarks, Simulators & Evaluation

- ★ **VLA-Arena-Live: Continually-Refreshed Factorial Evaluation**
  (arXiv:2606.00937; surfaced this period). The right
  benchmark-side contribution after CompoStack-VLA. VLA-Arena
  (2512.22539) is a fixed factorial grid, which means the
  composition pass risks overfitting to it. VLA-Arena-Live adds
  two structural pieces: (i) a *held-out* task pool refreshed
  monthly via procedural generation, and (ii) a *staleness score*
  that flags benchmark configurations on which the field's models
  have plateaued. The natural pair with WorldArena (2602.08971) on
  the WM side and the right answer to the gradient-leakage problem
  that afflicts every long-running embodied benchmark.

- ★ **DayHorizon-50: Multi-Hour Embodied Task Suite**
  (arXiv:2606.01617; same release as HierMem-VLA, surfaced this
  period). The first benchmark with multi-hour ground truth — 50
  tasks at 6–8 hour horizons spanning kitchen, household, and
  small-office settings, with structured sub-task milestones every
  10 minutes. The natural evaluation target for HierMem-VLA's
  day-scale semantic store and the right complement to RoboCerebra
  (2506.06677) at the upper end of the temporal scale.

- **SafetyVLA-Bench: Safety Evaluation Under Distribution Shift**
  (arXiv:2606.01744; same release as SafeVLA-Bound, surfaced this
  period). The empirical counterpart to SafeVLA-Bound. Five
  distribution-shift axes (object perturbation, lighting,
  instruction paraphrase, embodiment swap, novel distractor) with
  safety-margin annotations for each task. The right yardstick to
  decouple "safe under iid" from "safe under shift."

- **WorldArena** (arXiv:2602.08971; carried highlight). WM

- **VLA-Arena** (arXiv:2512.22539; carried highlight). Factorial

## 8. Applications: Navigation, Driving, Dexterous & Humanoid

- ★ **HumanoidStack-VLA: WholeBodyVLA + CompoStack on AgiBot X2**
  (arXiv:2606.02244; surfaced this period). The humanoid-side
  reading of CompoStack — the first paper to run the assembled
  post-training stack on a whole-body humanoid platform. The
  finding: the stack composes with WholeBodyVLA's whole-body
  control head, but PLD's residual probe head needs a
  *safety-constrained* variant for the legged-locomotion subspace.
  SafeVLA-Bound's separable safety penalty is the natural
  theoretical match. Reports 38.7% gain over WholeBodyVLA-alone on
  the AgiBot X2 long-horizon kitchen suite.

## Cross-Cutting Theme — composition results reorder the stack

The previous cycle (06-01) named the post-training stack as
*assembled*. This cycle's signal is that the **first composition
pass reorders the stack** and surfaces three new bottlenecks:

1. **Data > Objective > Substrate, empirically.** CompoStack-VLA
   (2606.02118) is the first end-to-end run, and the ranking is:
   PLD (data) +6.8 stand-alone; World2Act (objective) +4.9
   stand-alone but +8.3 when PLD is on; FPO + D-VLA (substrate)
   +2.1 and saturating. The honest reading: **data generation is
   the largest single lever; the objective is the largest
   compounding lever; the substrate is necessary infrastructure
   that doesn't compound.** This reorders the priority of the
   stack relative to what 06-01 implied.

2. **Safety bounds compose with performance bounds, separably.**
   SafeVLA-Bound (2606.01744) is the first VLA continual-RL bound
   that includes a safety margin *separably* — i.e. safety
   violations don't couple to the stability-plasticity trade-off.
   The right theoretical structure for production deployment and
   the cleanest answer to "is continual RL fine-tuning safe?" The
   bound's separability is the property that matters; it means the
   safety analysis can proceed component-by-component.

3. **Benchmarks need a live channel.** VLA-Arena-Live
   (2606.00937) is the first attempt at a benchmark that resists
   plateau by *refreshing* its task pool. The natural consequence
   of CompoStack-VLA's success: a fixed factorial grid will be
   solved within a quarter; the field needs an evaluation channel
   that doesn't go stale on that timescale.

Three secondary seams visible this cycle:

4. **Test-time WM use is underexploited.** WM-Critic-VLA
   (2606.02055) is the first paper to use the world model *at
   inference* as a critic. World2Act / World-Env / DUST all use
   the WM at training time; WM-Critic-VLA shows there's a free
   +5.3 on VLA-Arena Hard waiting at deployment. The composition
   with WM-augmented training is the obvious follow-up.

5. **Multi-hour horizons need structured memory, not bigger
   buffers.** HierMem-VLA (2606.01617) + DayHorizon-50 push the
   horizon from minutes to hours, and the finding is that the
   field's flat memory representations (MEM, EchoVLA) collapse
   beyond 90 minutes. The hierarchical structure
   (sensorimotor / episodic / semantic) is the right framing — the
   "memory bank" framing isn't.

6. **Multi-turn instruction following is a different problem from
   paraphrase robustness.** ContextVLA (2606.01876) makes explicit
   that LIBERO-Para-style paraphrase robustness is *within-turn*;
   real deployment needs *across-turn* referential resolution.
   The dialogue-state-tracking head is the right structural answer
   and a missing piece of the grounding line.

The honest summary: **the stack survives its first composition
pass; the next bottleneck is evaluation that doesn't go stale, a
safety bound that composes with the performance bound, and a
deployment use of the world model.** The empirical recipe is now
visible end-to-end; the theoretical and evaluation infrastructure
is the next thing to fill in.

## Open backlog for the next cycle (to watch)

- **CompoStack-VLA × WM-Critic-VLA — training-time + inference-time
  WM.** CompoStack uses the WM at training (via World2Act);
  WM-Critic-VLA uses it at inference. The natural experiment:
  compose them and report whether the +5.3 inference-time gain
  persists when the policy is *also* trained with World2Act. If
  yes, the WM is double-dipping productively; if no, the
  inference-time critic is recovering signal the training-time
  alignment already extracted.

- **SafeVLA-Bound on CompoStack's ablation table.** The natural
  extension of SafeVLA-Bound: apply the bound to each row of
  CompoStack-VLA's ablation table and report which component's
  contribution is safety-bounded vs. unbounded. The result would
  be the first principled answer to "which component is doing
  the dangerous work?"

- **VLA-Arena-Live's staleness score as a research signal.**
  VLA-Arena-Live flags benchmark configurations on which the field
  has plateaued. The natural follow-up: use the staleness score
  to direct the *research effort* — if Safety / Distractor /
  Extrapolation are all stale, the field knows where the next
  benchmark needs to push. This is the cleanest "benchmark guides
  research, not the other way around" framing yet.

- **HierMem-VLA's semantic store as a CRL-VLA-style bound.** The
  semantic store carries day-scale state; CRL-VLA's
  goal-conditioned advantage bound is the natural framing for
  what kind of *forgetting* the semantic store should permit. The
  bound would close the long-horizon stability question on
  HierMem-VLA's specific structure.

- **ContextVLA + Point-What-You-Mean — multi-turn external
  grounding.** ContextVLA handles across-turn referential
  resolution; Point-What-You-Mean handles within-turn external
  pointers. The combination — pointer-anchored multi-turn dialogue
  — is the obvious experiment for the deployment-grounding regime.

- **GRPO-VLA-v2 × PLD's residual probe head.** GRPO-VLA-v2's
  action-chunked group-relative advantages and PLD's residual
  probe head solve adjacent problems — credit assignment and
  curriculum, respectively. The composition is the natural
  RL-side experiment for the next cycle.
