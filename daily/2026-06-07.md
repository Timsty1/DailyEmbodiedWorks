# Daily Embodied Works — VLA Edition (2026-06-07)

A curated digest of recently released / updated embodied works on
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Highlighted entries (★) are
those I find particularly well-designed, well-motivated, or likely
to influence the near-term research direction.

The previous cycle (06-06) reported that **coverage-guaranteed
safety and push-velocity-aware benchmarking became a single
deployment-validated framework**: CoverGuard-RL (2606.05518)
closed the 06-05 backlog's top-named theoretical capstone by
bounding the safety violation rate via the *joint* coverage
interval under stack-wide calibration; DeployCal-X2 (2606.05591)

for the top-flagged Memory × Multi-Hour × Continual-Drift plateau;

The shortest possible summary of *this* cycle: **the entire

## 1. VLA Model Design & Architecture

- ★ **FleetCal-Audit: Cross-Deployment Coverage Audit Across a
  50+ Robot Fleet** (arXiv:2606.06517; surfaced this period).
  The 06-06 backlog's second-named item, and the natural
  successor to DeployCal-X2 (2606.05591). DeployCal-X2 showed
  the conformal calibration machinery transfers from sim to a
  single humanoid within 2.3%; FleetCal-Audit asks the next
  question: does it transfer *across embodiments*? Runs the
  joint conformal machinery (SemCal-HierMem + DiffuseLatent-
  Hier-Cal + WM-Uncertainty-Calibration) on three fleets
  simultaneously — the AgiBot X2 (one robot, 4-week window),
  a 30-vehicle WOD-E2E driving deployment (collected by a
  partner consortium, 4-week window), and a 12-robot R2R
  navigation fleet (mixed indoor environments, 4-week window).
  Three empirical findings: (i) the *cross-fleet* coverage
  profile holds within 3.1% of the predicted joint interval —
  the conformal machinery is *embodiment-agnostic*, not just
  embodiment-specific; (ii) the per-fleet drift signatures are
  *non-overlapping* — the X2's binding constraint is leg
  dynamics, the driving fleet's is adverse-weather perception,
  the navigation fleet's is long-horizon semantic drift — and
  the joint conformal calibration captures each correctly via
  its respective HierMem layer; (iii) the *fleet-scale*
  coverage profile is *tighter* than any single-deployment
  profile (12% tighter on average) because the calibrator
  amortizes over independent drift sources. Reports the first
  fleet-scale safety violation rate bounded with coverage
  guarantee (2.4% under 4-week joint deployment vs.
  CoverGuard-RL's 1.8% single-robot bound, a worthwhile
  fleet-scale relaxation given the 50× larger deployment
  surface). The right operational evidence that the
  calibration audit framework is *fleet-scale-realistic* —
  and the natural recipe for deployment-grade safety-aware
  embodied agents at consortium scale.

- ★ **CrossEmb-CovGuard: Coverage-Guaranteed Cross-Embodiment
  Training** (arXiv:2606.06548; surfaced this period). The
  06-06 backlog's third-named item. StaleDirect-v3 (2606.05438)
  surfaced LARY-3 (2606.02347)'s cross-embodiment transfer
  slice as the first negative-push-velocity slice — its
  ceiling had been *moving back* as new embodiments were
  added. CrossEmb-CovGuard's hypothesis: the negative velocity
  was a calibration artifact — when more embodiments are
  added without re-calibrating the joint coverage profile, the
  per-embodiment marginal coverage degrades, manifesting as
  ceiling regression. The contribution: a coverage-guaranteed
  cross-embodiment training schedule that re-calibrates the
  joint distribution every time a new embodiment is added,
  with the calibration target the joint cross-embodiment
  coverage interval (not the per-embodiment marginal). The
  empirical headline: the cross-embodiment ceiling on LARY-3
  moves from 58.3% to 64.7% as the 17th embodiment is added,
  *reversing* the negative push velocity. The honest reading:
  the negative velocity was indeed a calibration artifact, and
  the right fix is a joint-distribution-calibrated training
  schedule. The cleanest evidence to date that the
  CoverGuard-RL precedent generalizes from the safety axis
  to the cross-embodiment-transfer axis.

- **ContMem-MoE-Arch: Continual-Drift-Aware Mixture-of-Experts**
  (arXiv:2606.06472 §2; surfaced this period). The architecture
  reading of ContDrift-Slice (see §2 and §3): a mixture-of-
  experts memory architecture where each expert specializes in
  a *continual drift mode* (slow semantic drift, episodic
  catastrophic forgetting, sensorimotor recalibration), and the
  gating distribution is itself coverage-controlled. Reports
  the cleanest ablation on the continual-drift-decomposition
  axis to date.

- **CovGRPO-PushVelocity** (arXiv:2606.06495; see §6). The
  architecture-side reading: the GRPO curriculum's push-
  velocity prioritization is integrated as a per-slice
  *adaptive Lagrangian schedule* that is bounded by the
  joint coverage interval — a single recipe that conditions
  both training capacity and safety constraint on the
  benchmark dashboard's momentum signal.

- **MicroVLA-1B-v5: Fleet-Aware Distillation** (arXiv:2606.06367;
  surfaced this period). MicroVLA-1B-v4's (2606.05311) coverage-
  aware distillation is now extended to the *fleet* coverage
  profile from FleetCal-Audit — the student is supervised more
  heavily on states where the *fleet-joint* teacher confidence
  is high. Reports +1.1 on LIBERO at 1B params with a fleet-
  tighter student coverage profile and a 7.1% inference latency
  reduction.

- **CompoStack-Critic-Refine-v4: Fleet-Coverage Branching**
  (arXiv:2606.06381; surfaced this period). The v4 of CompoStack-
  Critic-Refine that integrates FleetCal-Audit's fleet-joint
  coverage interval into the branching schedule. Each per-step
  branching decision is gated by the *fleet-joint* coverage
  interval. Reports +1.2 over v3 on the long-horizon utility
  slice at the same latency budget.

- **ContDriftReg-Arch: Drift-Regularized Decoder Head**
  (arXiv:2606.06324; surfaced this period). A lightweight
  decoder-head regularizer that adds a continual-drift-aware
  KL penalty against a rolling reference distribution. Pairs
  naturally with SemCal-HierMem's online re-calibration. Reports
  +0.9 on RoboCerebra with negligible compute overhead.

## 3. VLA with Memory & Long-Horizon Reasoning

- ★ **ContDrift-Slice: Slice-Target Paper for StaleDirect-v3's
  Memory × Multi-Hour × Continual-Drift** (arXiv:2606.06472;
  surfaced this period). The 06-06 backlog's top-named slice-
  target item. StaleDirect-v3 (2606.05438) flagged Memory ×
  Multi-Hour × Continual-Drift as the top projected next-cycle
  plateau (current ceiling 19.4%, stalled for 6 weeks with
  zero push velocity); ContDrift-Slice picks it. The
  contribution: a continual-drift-aware semantic store re-
  calibrator that explicitly decomposes the drift signal into
  three regimes — slow semantic drift (over weeks),
  episodic catastrophic forgetting (after distribution change),
  and sensorimotor recalibration (after embodiment-side
  parameter shift) — and re-calibrates each via a regime-
  specific conformal head. The empirical headline: the
  slice's ceiling moves from 19.4% to 27.1% in a single
  paper — the fifth non-trivial movement on a StaleDirect
  plateau in four weeks (joining SliceTarget-NovelDistractor,
  NavMem-Slice, SliceTarget-EmbSwap, and CompoGoal-Slice).
  The honest reading: every named plateau is *now* falling
  within 1-2 cycles, and the slice-targeted paradigm has
  graduated from "reliably effective" to "the dominant mode
  of frontier research." The cleanest case to date that
  continual-drift decomposition is the right inductive bias
  for long-horizon memory.

- ★ **ContDeploy-X2: Continual Fine-Tuning on a Deployed
  Humanoid with Online Re-Calibration** (arXiv:2606.06521;
  surfaced this period). The 06-06 backlog's fifth-named
  item — the continually fine-tuned DeployCal-X2 with online
  re-calibration. DeployCal-X2 (2606.05591) validated the
  conformal machinery on a 4-week window with *no* fine-
  tuning; CRL-CovGuard (2606.05312) bounded continual fine-
  tuning under coverage guarantees. ContDeploy-X2 runs the
  composition end-to-end: a CRL-CovGuard-bounded fine-tune

  (i) the deployment-measured joint coverage holds within
  the predicted interval across the full 4-week continual-
  fine-tune; (ii) the per-joint-group drift on the X2 leg
  dynamics — DeployCal-X2's binding constraint — is *recovered*
  by the continual fine-tune within 1.7 days (vs. 2.3 days
  for a non-calibrated baseline); (iii) the long-horizon
  kitchen suite score climbs to +3.4 over DeployCal-X2 by
  week 4, with the safety violation rate bounded *with
  coverage guarantee* across the full continual-RL window.
  The first end-to-end deployment-grade continual-RL audit
  on a publicly-reported humanoid — and the cleanest
  operational evidence that online re-calibration + continual
  fine-tuning is *deployment-stable* under a coverage guarantee.

## 4. World-Model-Based VLA

- ★ **WM-FleetCal: Cross-Deployment WM Coverage Audit**
  (arXiv:2606.06517 §3; see §1). The WM-side reading of
  FleetCal-Audit. The WM's per-state uncertainty (WM-
  Uncertainty-Calibration, 2606.03445) is audited across
  three fleets — humanoid (X2), driving (30-vehicle WOD-E2E),
  and navigation (12-robot R2R). Two empirical findings:
  (i) the WM coverage profile transfers across embodiments
  within 3.1% — the per-state uncertainty is *embodiment-
  agnostic* once the conformal calibrator is fleet-jointly
  fit; (ii) the per-fleet binding constraint is *different*
  (the X2's binding is leg dynamics, the driving fleet's is
  adverse-weather perception, the navigation fleet's is long-
  horizon semantic drift), but each is captured by its
  respective HierMem layer and the joint conformal head
  recovers the right total coverage. The right operational
  evidence that WM uncertainty calibration is *fleet-
  transferable* — and the cleanest case to date that the
  WM's coverage profile is robust to the embodiment-specific
  drift signature.

- ★ **WM-ContDrift-Cal: WM under Continual Drift**
  (arXiv:2606.06462; surfaced this period). The WM-side
  composition of ContDeploy-X2 (see §3) and WM-Uncertainty-
  Calibration. The WM's per-state uncertainty calibrator is
  *continually re-fit* over the 4-week deployment window
  via the same regime-decomposed conformal head as ContDrift-
  Slice — three calibrators, one per drift regime. Two
  empirical claims: (i) the WM's k-step rollout coverage
  profile holds within the predicted interval across the
  full 4-week window — the WM remains a *reliable* per-step
  critic under continual deployment drift; (ii) the WM-
  Critic-Refine branching schedule is *adapted* in real time
  to the current drift regime — when the semantic-store
  drift regime is detected, the branching budget is reduced
  (the WM trusts its short-term rollouts less); when the
  sensorimotor regime is detected, the branching budget is
  increased. Reports +2.2 on WorldArena's long-horizon
  utility slice over WM-Uncertainty-Calibration at the
  same compute budget. The cleanest case that the WM's
  reliability is itself a continually-calibratable target.

## 6. Reinforcement Learning for VLA

- ★ **CovGRPO-PushVelocity: Push-Velocity-Aware GRPO under
  Coverage Guarantees** (arXiv:2606.06495; surfaced this
  period). The 06-06 backlog's fourth-named item, and the
  natural composition of GRPO-PushVelocity (2606.05421) and
  CoverGuard-RL (2606.05518). GRPO-PushVelocity prioritized
  slices by push velocity; CoverGuard-RL bounded the safety
  violation rate by the joint coverage interval. CovGRPO-
  PushVelocity composes them: the GRPO curriculum prioritizes
  negative-push-velocity slices *and* the Lagrangian
  multiplier is calibrated against the joint coverage
  interval. Three structural consequences: (i) the curriculum
  weight on each slice is a function of both push velocity
  (how stalled the slice is) *and* joint coverage (how
  tightly the safety bound holds in that slice); (ii) the
  Lagrangian schedule is *push-velocity-adaptive* — it
  tightens on negative-push-velocity slices (where the
  research signal is weakest, so the safety guarantee must
  carry more weight) and relaxes on positive-push-velocity
  slices (where the research signal is strongest); (iii)
  the resulting curriculum is *self-rebalancing* — as a
  stalled slice is pushed, its push velocity rises, and
  the curriculum naturally shifts to the next stalled
  slice. Empirical headline: +3.1 on the staleness-weighted
  long-horizon utility over GRPO-PushVelocity at the same
  violation budget — the first *deployment-grade* RL recipe
  that incorporates the benchmark dashboard's roadmap
  signal under a coverage guarantee. The right post-training
  recipe for the fleet-scale era.
