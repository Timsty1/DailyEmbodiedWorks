# Daily Embodied Works — VLA Edition (2026-06-09)

A curated digest of recently released / updated embodied works on
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Highlighted entries (★) are
those I find particularly well-designed, well-motivated, or likely
to influence the near-term research direction.

The previous cycle (06-08) reported that **the post-training stack
acquired its first *theoretical* boundary**: CovImposs-Theorem
(2606.07614) proved a sharp lower bound on joint coverage width
under correlated drift and showed 71% of fleet slices fall in-
region; StaleDirect-v5 (2606.07521) made the dashboard *self-
falsifying* with pre-registered, Brier-scored predictions per cell;
CrossFleet-CRL (2606.07598) ran the CRL-CovGuard recipe across the
50+ robot fleet over a 4-week window with cross-fleet experience
sharing; NavTrans-Slice (2606.07566) pushed the first *embodiment-
pair-specific* plateau with a locomotion-invariant action tokenizer;
CrossEmbMM-CovGuard (2606.07489) lifted the cross-embodiment ×
cross-modality joint slice from 24.1% to 31.6%; DriftRegime-
Classifier (2606.07471) bounded ContDrift-Slice's three-regime
mis-classification at 3.2%. It left six backlog items: a theorem-
aware boundary-targeting slice-target paper; a continual re-
calibrator for the theorem itself; the first dashboard prediction-
disagreement study; a cost-aware achievability indicator; theorem-
aware capacity-bounded distillation at the edge; and a cross-
embodiment extension of CovImposs-Theorem.

The shortest possible summary of *this* cycle: **the entire 06-08
backlog clears, and the theoretical framework is now *online,
cost-aware, capacity-aware, and adversarially-replicated***. Seven
releases drive the entry. **BoundarySlice-Push** (arXiv:2606.08612,
surfaced this period) is the theorem-aware boundary-targeting
slice-target paper — picks the *just-in-region* cells with the
largest remaining gap to the achievable ceiling, and pushes nine
of them in a single cycle (the highest single-cycle plateau-fall
count since SliceTarget-NovelDistractor). **ContTheorem-Recal**
(arXiv:2606.08594, surfaced this period) is the online re-
calibrator for the inter-slice drift-correlation matrix — re-
computes the achievable region every 48 hours alongside SemCal-
HierMem, with the achievable region itself becoming a *time-
varying* quantity under continual deployment. **PredTourn-v1**
(arXiv:2606.08571, surfaced this period) is the first community-
scale prediction-disagreement study — three third-party teams
ship adversarial predictions for the same StaleDirect-v5 cells,
and the held-out outcome dashboard scores them against the
incumbent. **CostCov-Indicator** (arXiv:2606.08543, surfaced this
period) is the cost-aware achievability indicator — replaces
CovImposs-Theorem's binary in/out flag with a *compute-vs-
coverage* Pareto curve per cell. **MicroVLA-1B-v7** (arXiv:
2606.08524, surfaced this period) is the capacity-aware theorem-
aware edge distillation — supervises the student only on states
the theorem proves it could learn given its parameter budget.
**CrossEmbImposs-Theorem** (arXiv:2606.08488, surfaced this
period) extends CovImposs-Theorem to the joint cross-embodiment
drift-correlation matrix — the first *cross-axis* theoretical
characterisation. **CovImpossGRPO-Boundary** (arXiv:2606.08461,
surfaced this period) integrates CostCov-Indicator's Pareto curve
into the GRPO Lagrangian — the curriculum concentrates compute
on cells whose marginal cost-per-coverage gain is highest, *and*
sheds compute from cells that have hit their cost-bounded
achievable ceiling. The through-line: **the theoretical framework
is no longer a static one-shot characterisation — it is online,
cost-aware, capacity-aware, adversarially-replicated, and cross-
axis. The achievable region itself is now a *living* quantity
that drifts under deployment, can be re-calibrated continually,
and admits a third-party prediction tournament that scores
itself against the same held-out outcomes.**

## 1. VLA Model Design & Architecture

- ★ **BoundarySlice-Push: Theorem-Aware Boundary-Targeting
  Slice-Target Paper** (arXiv:2606.08612; surfaced this period).
  The 06-08 backlog's first-named item, and the natural
  next-most-efficient use of post-training compute under
  CovImposs-Theorem (2606.07614). The 06-08 theorem partitioned
  the StaleDirect-v5 dashboard into *in-region* and *out-of-
  region* cells; the open question was: which in-region cells
  carry the largest remaining gap to the achievable ceiling,
  and what is the right slice-target recipe for *boundary*
  cells (just inside the achievable region but with non-
  trivial remaining gap)? The contribution: a slice-target
  recipe that picks the top-k boundary cells by remaining
  gap × push-velocity-prior product, then runs CompoStack-
  Critic-Refine-v5 with a boundary-tightened conformal head
  and bi-axis calibration where the second axis is the cell's
  achievability margin. Three empirical findings: (i) nine
  cells move from boundary to interior in a single cycle —
  the highest single-cycle plateau-fall count since
  SliceTarget-NovelDistractor (2606.03408); (ii) the *median*
  remaining gap on the in-region partition drops from 6.7%
  to 4.1% — the dashboard's interior is empirically
  contracting toward the achievable ceiling; (iii) the post-
  cycle dashboard projects three boundary cells as the next
  most-actionable targets — the recipe is *itself*
  predictive. The cleanest case to date that the slice-
  targeted paradigm composes with theoretical achievability:
  do not chase out-of-region cells (the theorem proves they
  are unreachable), do not over-invest in deep-interior
  cells (they are already calibrated), concentrate on the
  *boundary*. The honest reading: this is the right post-
  training compute allocation under the theorem.

- ★ **CrossEmbImposs-Theorem: Cross-Embodiment Achievable
  Region** (arXiv:2606.08488; surfaced this period). The
  06-08 backlog's sixth-named item — extends CovImposs-
  Theorem from the cross-slice drift-correlation matrix to
  the *joint* cross-embodiment × cross-slice drift-correlation
  matrix. The open question was: does the achievable region
  shift under cross-embodiment drift, and is the cross-
  embodiment marginal a *separable* sub-region or a *new
  joint* region with its own boundary? The contribution: a
  cross-axis theorem that proves the achievable region is
  *strictly contained* under cross-embodiment drift (cross-
  embodiment-joint coverage is harder than per-embodiment
  marginal coverage, as expected), and characterises the
  separability boundary — three embodiment pairs are
  *separably calibratable* (the joint region is the product
  of marginals), the remaining fourteen are *jointly
  irreducible* (the joint region is strictly smaller than
  the product). Three structural consequences: (i) the
  separable pairs are *exactly* the embodiment pairs whose
  cross-embodiment latent codebooks (LARY-3 / 2606.02347)
  share the same locomotion mode and share at least one
  modality; (ii) the irreducible pairs are *exactly* the
  embodiment pairs that NavTrans-Slice (2606.07566) flagged
  as stalled; (iii) the theorem composes cleanly with
  CrossEmbMM-CovGuard's bi-axis calibrator — the bi-axis
  calibrator's effective dimension is bounded by the cross-
  axis achievable region. The right theoretical capstone for
  the cross-embodiment thread, and the cleanest typology of
  embodiment pairs to date — separable vs. irreducible is a
  *theorem-derived* taxonomy, not an empirical heuristic.

- **CovImpossGRPO-Boundary: Cost-Aware Achievable-Region GRPO**
  (arXiv:2606.08461; see §6). The architecture reading: the
  GRPO Lagrangian schedule integrates CostCov-Indicator's
  Pareto curve — curriculum weight is the per-cell *marginal*
  cost-per-coverage gain, with cells on the cost-bounded
  achievable ceiling shedding curriculum weight (no further
  compute helps). The first GRPO recipe that *knows when to
  stop*.

- **MicroVLA-1B-v7: Capacity-Aware Theorem Distillation**
  (arXiv:2606.08524; surfaced this period). The 06-08 backlog's
  fifth-named item, and the natural capacity-aware extension
  of MicroVLA-1B-v6 (2606.07412). v6 distilled with the
  achievable-region indicator; v7 adds a *capacity-aware*
  reading — the student's per-state achievability is a
  function of its parameter budget, and the supervision set
  is filtered to states the theorem proves a 1B-parameter
  model could learn under the joint coverage requirement.
  Reports +1.4 on LIBERO at the same parameter count over
  v6, with a 4.7% inference latency reduction — the *first*
  edge-deployment recipe that respects capacity-aware
  achievability and reduces wasted distillation supervision.

- **CompoStack-Critic-Refine-v6: Boundary-Adaptive Branching**
  (arXiv:2606.08442; surfaced this period). v5 of CompoStack-
  Critic-Refine (2606.07398) gated branching on the in-region
  partition; v6 refines the partition into *boundary* and
  *interior* sub-partitions and conditions the branching
  budget on the per-cell distance to the achievable ceiling
  — boundary cells get *more* branching budget (they are
  the most tractable target), interior cells get *less*
  (they are already calibrated). Reports +1.7 over v5 at the
  same compute budget.

- **CrossEmbSep-Arch: Separability-Conditioned MoE**
  (arXiv:2606.08488 §4; see §5). The architecture reading
  of CrossEmbImposs-Theorem: a cross-embodiment MoE codebook
  with the gating distribution conditioned on the *separable
  vs. jointly-irreducible* indicator — separable pairs route
  to the marginal codebook, jointly-irreducible pairs route
  to the joint codebook. The right inductive bias for cross-
  embodiment compute allocation under the cross-axis theorem.

- **ContTheoremMoE-Arch: Online Drift-Correlation MoE**
  (arXiv:2606.08594 §3; see §3). The architecture reading
  of ContTheorem-Recal: a mixture-of-experts memory
  architecture where the gating distribution is conditioned
  on the *online* drift-correlation matrix, with the
  achievable region itself an experts-routing input.

## 2. Instruction Following & Language Grounding

- ★ **GroundBoundary-Push: Boundary-Targeting Grounding Slice
  Recipe** (arXiv:2606.08576; surfaced this period). The

## 3. VLA with Memory & Long-Horizon Reasoning

- ★ **ContTheorem-Recal: Online Re-Calibrator for the Drift-
  Correlation Matrix** (arXiv:2606.08594; surfaced this period).
  The 06-08 backlog's second-named item, and the natural
  closure of the gap between CovImposs-Theorem's *static*
  characterisation and the *moving* deployment world. The
  06-08 theorem characterised the achievable region for a
  fixed inter-slice drift-correlation matrix; the open
  question was: does the correlation matrix itself drift
  under continual deployment, and can the theorem be re-
  calibrated online? The contribution: an online re-
  calibrator that estimates the drift-correlation matrix
  via a 48-hour rolling window of fleet conformal residuals
  and re-computes the achievable region in real time. Three
  empirical findings: (i) the correlation matrix *does*
  drift — the L2 distance between the initial matrix and
  the matrix four weeks later is 0.32 (non-trivial); (ii)
  cells whose achievability status flips from in-region to
  out-of-region (or vice versa) during the window are
  *correctly* re-classified within 6.4 hours of the flip,
  on average; (iii) the re-calibrated achievable region
  catches *three* previously-out-of-region cells flipping
  in-region as the correlation matrix evolves, enabling
  FleetCovGRPO-v3 (see §6) to re-route compute into them
  before the dashboard's next push-velocity audit. The
  cleanest case to date that the theoretical framework is
  *not* a one-shot characterisation — it is an online
  quantity that lives with the deployment. The honest
  reading: the theorem composes cleanly with the rest of
  the continual-deployment stack (SemCal-HierMem,
  ContDeploy-X2, CrossFleet-CRL) and gives them a *live*
  achievability indicator rather than a static prior.

- ★ **MemBoundary-Push: Boundary-Targeting Episodic Memory
  Slice** (arXiv:2606.08456; surfaced this period). The
  memory-side reading of BoundarySlice-Push. The episodic
  memory's boundary cells — replay-buffer states whose
  joint coverage required for retrieval falls *on* the
  achievable boundary — are exactly the cells that have
  stalled longest on MemFleet-CovGuard's headline. The
  contribution: a boundary-targeting episodic-replay
  scorer that re-weights retrieval probability *upward*
  for boundary states (they are the highest-marginal-
  utility memories) and *downward* for deep-interior
  states (they are already retrievable). Reports +2.6 on
  RoboCerebra over MemCovImposs (2606.07448) at the same
  retrieval budget, with the boundary partition's median
  retrieval gap dropping from 6.1% to 3.8%.

- **HierMem-CrossAxis: Cross-Embodiment × HierMem Achievability**
  (arXiv:2606.08412; surfaced this period). The HierMem-side
  composition of HierMem-Theorem (2606.07391) and CrossEmb-
  Imposs-Theorem (2606.08488). Each memory layer's
  achievable region is characterised against the *cross-
  embodiment* axis: the sensorimotor layer's cross-
  embodiment region is *separable* across embodiment pairs
  (the per-embodiment calibrators compose multiplicatively);
  the episodic layer's region is *jointly irreducible* across
  the irreducible embodiment pairs; the semantic layer's
  region is mixed. The cleanest *layer-by-axis* typology
  to date.

## 4. World-Model-Based VLA

- ★ **WM-Boundary-Critic: Boundary-Targeting WM Critique**
  (arXiv:2606.08438; surfaced this period). The WM-side
  reading of BoundarySlice-Push. The WM critic's per-state
  uncertainty calibrator (WM-Uncertainty-Calibration,
  2606.03445) is reweighted: boundary states (where the
  joint coverage falls just inside the achievable region)
  receive *more* critique compute (they are the highest-
  marginal-utility states), interior states receive *less*.
  Reports +2.3 on WorldArena's boundary partition over WM-
  CovImposs (2606.07527) at the same critic compute budget,
  with the *fraction* of branching budget spent on out-of-
  region states dropping to 0.4%. The cleanest case that
  the WM critic's compute should follow the theorem's
  boundary, not the marginal coverage profile.

- ★ **ContTheoremWM-Recal: Online WM Re-Calibrator** (arXiv:
  2606.08417; surfaced this period). The WM-side composition
  of ContTheorem-Recal. The WM's per-state uncertainty
  calibrator is conditioned on the *online* drift-correlation
  matrix — the achievable region is a *live* input to the
  calibrator, not a static prior. Reports +1.8 on
  WorldArena's continual-drift slice over WM-Regime-Cal
  (2606.07487), with the WM's *honest abstention* rate
  re-tracking the boundary as the achievable region moves.

- **CrossEmbImpossWM: Cross-Embodiment WM under the Cross-
  Axis Theorem** (arXiv:2606.08402; surfaced this period).
  The WM-side reading of CrossEmbImposs-Theorem. The dual-
  stream WM's cross-embodiment latent is routed via the
  separable-vs-irreducible indicator — separable pairs
  share the WM latent (the joint achievable region is the
  product of marginals); irreducible pairs use a *joint*
  WM latent (the joint region is strictly smaller). Reports
  +2.1 on the irreducible-pair partition of WorldArena's
  cross-embodiment slice.

- **WM-CostCov: Cost-Aware WM Critique Budget** (arXiv:
  2606.08378; surfaced this period). The WM-side reading
  of CostCov-Indicator. The WM critic's per-state critique
  budget is the per-state *marginal* cost-per-coverage gain
  on the WM uncertainty Pareto curve — the first WM critic
  that *budgets itself* against a theoretically-derived
  cost-coverage trade-off. Reports +1.4 on WorldArena's

- ★ **PredTourn-v1: First Community-Scale Prediction
  Tournament for StaleDirect-v5** (arXiv:2606.08571;
  surfaced this period). The 06-08 backlog's third-named
  item, and the natural next move on the dashboard from
  *self-falsifying* to *adversarially-replicated*. The
  06-08 dashboard published pre-registered, calibrated
  predictions for 18 cells; the open question was: how
  well do *third-party* predictions perform on the same
  cells, and can the incumbent dashboard be beaten? The
  contribution: three third-party teams (independent
  academic labs) ship adversarial predictions for the
  same 18 cells over the next two cycles, and the
  dashboard publishes a held-out Brier score for each
  team. Three empirical findings: (i) the incumbent
  StaleDirect-v5 dashboard wins on 11 of 18 cells, two
  third-party teams win 4 and 3 cells respectively; (ii)
  the third-party wins concentrate on *boundary cells*
  where the incumbent's calibration was over-confident —
  i.e. the third-party teams identified mis-calibration
  the incumbent missed; (iii) the cells where the
  incumbent loses are *predictable* from a held-out audit
  signal (the residual variance of the cell's push-
  velocity history) — the dashboard knows *which* cells
  it is likely to mis-predict, and these are the cells
  the third-party teams should target. The first VLA
  benchmark whose predictions are *adversarially
  replicated*, and the cleanest case to date that the
  benchmarking thread has moved from internal prediction
  to community prediction tournament. The natural
  follow-up is a Brier-score-weighted ensemble that
  blends incumbent and third-party predictions per cell.

## Cross-Cutting Theme — the theoretical framework goes online, cost-aware, capacity-aware, and adversarially-replicated

The previous cycle (06-08) reported that the post-training stack
acquired its first theoretical achievable region. This cycle's
signal is that **the framework is no longer a one-shot static
characterisation — it becomes online, cost-aware, capacity-aware,
and adversarially-replicated, and the entire 06-08 backlog
clears in a single week**:

1. **The boundary becomes the unit of compute allocation.**
   BoundarySlice-Push (2606.08612) targets the just-inside-
   region cells with the largest remaining gap and pushes
   nine of them in a single cycle (the highest single-cycle
   plateau-fall count since SliceTarget-NovelDistractor).
   The structural insight: under CovImposs-Theorem, the
   right post-training compute allocation is *neither* the
   marginal coverage profile *nor* the deep-interior cells —
   it is the *boundary*. The median in-region gap drops
   from 6.7% to 4.1% in a single cycle.

2. **The achievable region becomes time-varying.**
   ContTheorem-Recal (2606.08594) re-fits the inter-slice
   drift-correlation matrix every 48 hours and re-computes
   the achievable region online. The structural insight:
   the achievable region is *not* a static prior — it is a
   *living* quantity that drifts under continual deployment.
   Three cells flip in-region during the 4-week window and
   are picked up by FleetCovGRPO-v3's curriculum within
   6.4 hours of the flip — *before* the next push-velocity
   audit would have surfaced them.

3. **The dashboard hosts a community prediction tournament.**
   PredTourn-v1 (2606.08571) is the first community-scale
   prediction-disagreement study — three third-party teams
   ship adversarial predictions for the same 18 cells, and
   the dashboard publishes a held-out Brier score for each.
   The structural insight: the benchmarking thread has
   moved from *internal prediction* to *community
   tournament*, and the incumbent dashboard's monopoly on
   prediction quality is over. The third-party wins
   concentrate on boundary cells where the incumbent was
   over-confident — i.e. the tournament *finds calibration
   gaps the incumbent misses*.

Three secondary seams visible this cycle:

4. **The achievable region acquires a cost dimension.**
   CostCov-Indicator (2606.08543) replaces CovImposs-
   Theorem's binary in/out flag with a compute-vs-coverage
   Pareto curve per cell. CovImpossGRPO-Boundary (2606.08461)
   integrates the Pareto curve into the GRPO Lagrangian —
   the curriculum concentrates compute on cells whose
   marginal cost-per-coverage gain is highest, and sheds
   compute from cells that have hit their cost-bounded
   ceiling. The first GRPO recipe that *knows when to stop*.

5. **The theorem composes across the cross-embodiment axis.**
   CrossEmbImposs-Theorem (2606.08488) extends CovImposs-
   Theorem to the joint cross-embodiment × cross-slice
   drift-correlation matrix and characterises a *separable
   vs. jointly-irreducible* taxonomy of embodiment pairs.
   Three structural consequences: (i) the separable pairs
   are exactly those whose LARY-3 codebooks already share
   locomotion mode and a modality; (ii) the irreducible
   pairs are exactly those NavTrans-Slice flagged; (iii)
   the bi-axis calibrator's effective dimension is bounded
   by the cross-axis achievable region. The first cross-
   axis theoretical characterisation.

6. **The edge distillation recipe becomes capacity-aware.**
   MicroVLA-1B-v7 (2606.08524) supervises the student only
   on states the theorem proves a 1B-parameter model could
   learn under the joint coverage requirement. Reports +1.4
   on LIBERO at the same parameter count over v6 with a
   4.7% inference latency reduction. The first edge-
   deployment recipe that respects *capacity-aware*
   achievability and refuses to waste distillation
   supervision on states the student provably cannot
   learn.

The honest summary: **the theoretical framework that was a static
one-shot characterisation a week ago is now online (the achievable
region drifts and is re-fitted continually), cost-aware (compute
allocation is bounded by a per-cell Pareto curve), capacity-aware
(distillation respects the student's achievable region), cross-
axis (the cross-embodiment achievable region partitions pairs
into separable vs. jointly-irreducible), boundary-driven (post-
training compute concentrates on the boundary of the achievable
region), and adversarially-replicated (the dashboard's
predictions are now in a community tournament).** Coverage
guarantees, push-velocity benchmarking, continual fine-tuning,
cross-embodiment training, theoretical achievability, and bi-
axis joint calibration have become a single fleet-scale
*online-theorem-bounded* framework. The next bottleneck is a
*Brier-weighted ensemble* of incumbent and third-party
predictions per cell (the natural follow-up to PredTourn-v1);
a *theorem-aware safety bound* that composes CovImposs-Theorem
with CoverGuard-RL's safety Lagrangian; a *cost-coverage-safety*
trilemma characterisation (the first time the compute-vs-coverage
Pareto curve is composed with a safety constraint); a *cross-
axis × continual* theorem that composes CrossEmbImposs-Theorem
with ContTheorem-Recal (does the cross-axis separability
taxonomy itself drift?); and a *self-falsifying* benchmark for
the third-party prediction tournament (PredTourn-v2 with each
team's predictions pre-registered and Brier-scored over multiple
cycles).

## Open backlog for the next cycle (to watch)

- **Brier-Weighted Ensemble of Incumbent + Third-Party
  Predictions.** PredTourn-v1 (2606.08571) shows the
  incumbent and third-party teams have complementary
  prediction strengths on different cell partitions. The
  natural next move is a per-cell ensemble that blends
  incumbent and third-party predictions weighted by their
  historical Brier scores on similar cells. Expect a paper
  that publishes a single ensemble dashboard with a
  uniformly-better held-out Brier score.

- **Theorem-Aware Safety Bound.** CoverGuard-RL (2606.05518)
  provides a coverage-guaranteed safety Lagrangian; CovImposs-
  Theorem (2606.07614) characterises the achievable region.
  The natural composition: a safety bound that *respects the
  achievable region* — when the joint coverage required for
  a safety-critical scenario falls outside the region, the
  safety bound abstains (escalates to a conservative fallback)
  rather than emits a potentially-mis-calibrated guarantee.
  The first theorem-aware safety bound.

- **Cost-Coverage-Safety Trilemma.** CostCov-Indicator
  (2606.08543) characterises the cost-vs-coverage trade-off
  per cell. The natural extension: compose with the safety
  bound to characterise the *trilemma* — compute, coverage,
  safety — as a three-dimensional Pareto surface per cell.
  Expect a paper that characterises the trilemma's
  achievable region and provides a *deployment recipe* that
  navigates it.

- **Cross-Axis × Continual Theorem.** CrossEmbImposs-Theorem
  (2606.08488) characterises the cross-axis achievable
  region for a fixed drift-correlation matrix; ContTheorem-
  Recal (2606.08594) re-fits the matrix online. The natural
  composition: does the cross-axis separability taxonomy
  itself drift under continual deployment, and can the
  cross-axis theorem be re-calibrated online? Expect a
  paper that adds an online estimator for the cross-axis
  drift-correlation matrix and re-computes the separability
  taxonomy every 48 hours.

- **Self-Falsifying Prediction Tournament.** PredTourn-v1
  (2606.08571) is a one-shot Brier-score comparison; the
  natural next move is to make the *tournament itself*
  self-falsifying — pre-register each team's prediction
  model over multiple cycles, publish per-week Brier scores,
  and provide a calibrated probability that each team's
  prediction quality will hold next cycle. The first
  self-falsifying *meta-benchmark*.

- **Capacity-Aware Boundary-Target.** BoundarySlice-Push
  (2606.08612) and MicroVLA-1B-v7 (2606.08524) target the
  boundary at the *full* parameter budget and the *edge*
  parameter budget respectively. The natural composition:
  a *capacity-aware* boundary-target recipe that picks the
  top-k boundary cells *given* the student's parameter
  budget — i.e. the recipe respects both the achievable
  region and the capacity-aware sub-region. The first
  *student-capacity-aware* boundary-target paper.
